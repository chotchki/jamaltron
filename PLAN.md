# PLAN

Jamaltron: a Factorio 2.1.x mod adding a spidertron variant that is Jamal, the spider-legged hammerhead shark from *Dungeon Crawler Carl* (books 7 and 8). What and why live in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md), this is the how. Three tracks that only meet at the entity prototype:

- character: book text → per-book profiles → one canonical profile → a situation-to-line catalog, the SAME pipeline as an earlier private character project (extractors already exist there, we reuse them)
- art: the bought hammerhead model → Blender headless → Factorio sprite sheets (64 rotations, shadow, flop loop). Only the renders get committed, the source model NEVER does
- mod: Lua, cloned from the stock spidertron, plus a scripted jump that can break his legs

The jump is the one genuine unknown (spider-vehicles don't jump, so it's a teleport or an entity swap and the spike in E.1 decides). Everything else is known-shape work with a headless smoke test (`factorio --create`) as the gate.

BUDGET DECIDED 2026-09-20 (chotchki: "thrashing variety would be wise"), and the key finding is that DIRECTIONS are the expensive axis and buy the LEAST variety - 8 directions of one loop costs 95 MiB body + 190 shadow and the player still sees the identical thrash every time, because repetition is noticed in TIME not in heading. So: ONE direction, and a LONG cycle with genuinely different beats - heave, settle, weak twitch, pause, snap at nothing, heave - at 60 frames x 1 dir = 23.8 MiB, the same budget as his standing body and 2.5s at 24fps. A clean 20-frame loop reads as a machine; varied beats read as a distressed animal. UPGRADE PATH if it wears out in play: 2-3 beached PROTOTYPES picked at random on break (24-36 MiB), which is nearly free since D.4 already chooses a swap target. FREE SECOND-ORDER WIN: the `flopping` line pool fires on its own timer, so a 2.5s visual cycle against a different line period DRIFTS - the same flop frame lands under different lines each time round. TECHNIQUE (chotchki 2026-09-20, and it is the insight that makes C.5 cheap): the shipped swim clip is a TRAVELLING WAVE - the bend moves head-to-tail, which is precisely what reads as propulsion and is why a rolled swim cycle looks like a shark swimming sideways rather than a fish dying on a beach. A beached fish makes a STANDING wave: head and tail bend the SAME way together, the body curls into a U, then snaps the other way. Same bones, same keyframes, DIFFERENT PHASE RELATIONSHIP. So split the spine's fcurves into a front group and a back group and play them from different start offsets - roughly half a period brings head and tail into phase and collapses the travelling wave into the U. Two NLA strips with a time offset per bone group, no new animation authored. SPIKE MEASURED 2026-09-20 (scratchpad/c5wave): chotchki's standing-wave idea SURVIVES - SWIM_FAST is a textbook travelling wave, lag a dead-uniform 1.50 frames/bone, head-to-tail EXACTLY 10.000 frames = half the period, so head and tail already sit in perfect anti-phase and re-phasing is the right lever. BUT half a period is the answer for a UNIFORM-amplitude wave and this one is REAR-LOADED 3:1 (front four joints 12.8 deg, rear four 37.7), so the amplitude-weighted offset is k=4..6 and k=10 measures WORSE on every metric. Best of all is no grouping: cancel each bone's OWN measured lag (0/1.50/3.00/4.49/5.49/6.99/8.50/10.00), standing_R 0.999 vs 0.976 for the best 2-group split, same cost and no seam. PHASE ALONE IS ONLY ~30% OF A THRASH - at shipped amplitude no k reads as one (peak curl 36-49 deg over a 3.6-unit body is a gentle bow). Three more, NONE needing new keyframes: (1) BODY-FRAME RECENTRING, the biggest visual change and a RIG artefact - SPINE_01 is a root bone so 4.2 units of shark hang off a pinned nose and it reads as head-bolted-to-floor; (2) mid-peaked amplitude ~15 deg/joint, uniform gain just amplifies the rear-loading into a scorpion tail; (3) ground contact at z=0 plus a retime that lingers at the extremes and snaps through the middle, because a sinusoid is the signature of something driven and comfortable. All three swim clips are the SAME wave at three speeds (lag/P = 0.500/0.500/0.499), so SWIM_SLOW buys time resolution not new motion. BOUNCE (chotchki 2026-09-20), and it corrects the spike's own recommendation: the spike said PIN the lowest point to z=0, which makes him SLIDE. A real beached fish thrashes hard enough to launch itself and LAND, and the landing is where the comedy is. Animate a per-frame vertical lift driven by the thrash phase - push off at peak curl, fall back, land. Pure root transform, no rig work. THE PAYOFF IS THE SHADOW: at the C.3-derived sun (45 deg, run 1.0) a 0.3-tile lift separates the shadow 0.3 tiles = 19 px east while the body rises 14 px up-screen, and that SEPARATION is the cue that reads as airborne - a lift without it just looks like the sprite growing. It is also FREE: the shadow pass is a real Cycles render with a real sun and a catcher at z=0, so lifting the model makes the shadow land correctly by itself rather than us applying an offset. COSTS: the frame box grows by the lift in world z (he is already ~4 tiles tall posed), and the landing frames are the sync points for D.6's wet slap and any E.3-style dust, which is a tighter coupling than the apology timer needs - the LINES should drift against the loop, the SLAP should not. *** THE PLAN'S OWN PREMISE IS WRONG, measured 2026-09-20 (scratchpad/c5/sheets). "Re-posed on its side" is exactly the failure mode: ROLL 90 IS THE THING THAT KILLS THE FLOP. At the broadside directions - the only ones where a shark on the ground reads as being ON the ground - the swim's bend plane IS the model's transverse plane, so rolling 90 stands that plane perpendicular to the screen, the bend foreshortens into depth, and you get eight nearly identical frames. Sheet H (gain sweep at roll 90) goes to mush; sheet B shows past roll 105 he reads as dead-belly-up-in-water rather than beached. WHAT WORKS: SWIM_FAST at ROLL ~30 with POSE GAIN 2.0-2.6x. The head is nailed down (no swim clip touches HEAD at all) while the body whips through a big SCREEN-PLANE arc off it, which reads as a fish thrashing against something that has hold of it. The rig survives 2.6x with no mesh tearing and no candy-wrapper collapse at the neck. THE MISSING KNOB IS TWO KNOBS: `model.action` alone only gets you the roll-90 picture that does not work; `model.pose_gain` is what makes the bought clip usable at all. Recipe: SWIM_FAST, roll 30, gain 2.0, frame stride 2 (10-frame 0.42s loop), BITE_01 amplified underneath for the pectoral fin - the two families animate DISJOINT bones so they compose for free (rendered together, sheet E). NOTE the two spikes converged independently on AMPLITUDE as the missing ingredient: the phase spike said mid-peaked ~15 deg/joint, this one says gain 2.0-2.6x. *** CHOTCHKI OVERRULED THE SPIKE 2026-09-20 after looking at the sheets: ROLL 80-90 WITH THE NORMAL (ungained) ANIMATION. His eye over the agent's metric, and both readings are defensible because they measure different things - the agent measured TAIL-TIP TRAVEL ON SCREEN over stills and it explicitly did not verify anything temporal. At roll 0 the swim's bend plane is horizontal and fully visible from above; at roll 90 it stands VERTICAL and foreshortens by 0.707 - reduced, NOT eliminated. THE CONSEQUENCE THAT MAKES 80-90 WORK: at that roll the bend rotates into the same plane as the BOUNCE, so curl-up / lift-off / slam-down is ONE composed motion instead of two independent effects. The bounce stops being decoration and becomes the thing that carries the thrash, which is why ungained reads fine here and needed gain 2.0 at roll 30. Keep roll under ~105 - past that sheet B shows him reading as dead-belly-up-in-water rather than beached.
