# PLAN

Jamaltron: a Factorio 2.1.x mod adding a spidertron variant that is Jamal, the spider-legged hammerhead shark from *Dungeon Crawler Carl* (books 7 and 8). What and why live in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md), this is the how. Three tracks that only meet at the entity prototype:

- character: book text → per-book profiles → one canonical profile → a situation-to-line catalog, the SAME pipeline as an earlier private character project (extractors already exist there, we reuse them)
- art: the bought hammerhead model → Blender headless → Factorio sprite sheets (64 rotations, shadow, flop loop). Only the renders get committed, the source model NEVER does
- mod: Lua, cloned from the stock spidertron, plus a scripted jump that can break his legs

The jump is the one genuine unknown (spider-vehicles don't jump, so it's a teleport or an entity swap and the spike in E.1 decides). Everything else is known-shape work with a headless smoke test (`factorio --create`) as the gate.

- [ ] C.5 - Beached + airborne sheets: swim cycle re-posed on its side → flopping loop for `jamaltron-beached`; pitched-up body rotations for the jump arc. BUDGET DECIDED 2026-09-20 (chotchki: "thrashing variety would be wise"), and the key finding is that DIRECTIONS are the expensive axis and buy the LEAST variety - 8 directions of one loop costs 95 MiB body + 190 shadow and the player still sees the identical thrash every time, because repetition is noticed in TIME not in heading. So: ONE direction, and a LONG cycle with genuinely different beats - heave, settle, weak twitch, pause, snap at nothing, heave - at 60 frames x 1 dir = 23.8 MiB, the same budget as his standing body and 2.5s at 24fps. A clean 20-frame loop reads as a machine; varied beats read as a distressed animal. UPGRADE PATH if it wears out in play: 2-3 beached PROTOTYPES picked at random on break (24-36 MiB), which is nearly free since D.4 already chooses a swap target. FREE SECOND-ORDER WIN: the `flopping` line pool fires on its own timer, so a 2.5s visual cycle against a different line period DRIFTS - the same flop frame lands under different lines each time round. TECHNIQUE (chotchki 2026-09-20, and it is the insight that makes C.5 cheap): the shipped swim clip is a TRAVELLING WAVE - the bend moves head-to-tail, which is precisely what reads as propulsion and is why a rolled swim cycle looks like a shark swimming sideways rather than a fish dying on a beach. A beached fish makes a STANDING wave: head and tail bend the SAME way together, the body curls into a U, then snaps the other way. Same bones, same keyframes, DIFFERENT PHASE RELATIONSHIP. So split the spine's fcurves into a front group and a back group and play them from different start offsets - roughly half a period brings head and tail into phase and collapses the travelling wave into the U. Two NLA strips with a time offset per bone group, no new animation authored. SPIKE MEASURED 2026-09-20 (scratchpad/c5wave): chotchki's standing-wave idea SURVIVES - SWIM_FAST is a textbook travelling wave, lag a dead-uniform 1.50 frames/bone, head-to-tail EXACTLY 10.000 frames = half the period, so head and tail already sit in perfect anti-phase and re-phasing is the right lever. BUT half a period is the answer for a UNIFORM-amplitude wave and this one is REAR-LOADED 3:1 (front four joints 12.8 deg, rear four 37.7), so the amplitude-weighted offset is k=4..6 and k=10 measures WORSE on every metric. Best of all is no grouping: cancel each bone's OWN measured lag (0/1.50/3.00/4.49/5.49/6.99/8.50/10.00), standing_R 0.999 vs 0.976 for the best 2-group split, same cost and no seam. PHASE ALONE IS ONLY ~30% OF A THRASH - at shipped amplitude no k reads as one (peak curl 36-49 deg over a 3.6-unit body is a gentle bow). Three more, NONE needing new keyframes: (1) BODY-FRAME RECENTRING, the biggest visual change and a RIG artefact - SPINE_01 is a root bone so 4.2 units of shark hang off a pinned nose and it reads as head-bolted-to-floor; (2) mid-peaked amplitude ~15 deg/joint, uniform gain just amplifies the rear-loading into a scorpion tail; (3) ground contact at z=0 plus a retime that lingers at the extremes and snaps through the middle, because a sinusoid is the signature of something driven and comfortable. All three swim clips are the SAME wave at three speeds (lag/P = 0.500/0.500/0.499), so SWIM_SLOW buys time resolution not new motion
