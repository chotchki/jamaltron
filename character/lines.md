# Jamal — situation → line catalog

This file is the SINGLE source of truth for every string the mod speaks. `scripts/lines.lua` and `locale/en/jamaltron-lines.cfg` are GENERATED from it by `tools/gen_lines.py` (PLAN B.5), and both carry a header saying so. Never hand-edit the generated files: the next generator run silently eats the edit, and the diff won't tell you which side is right.

Built from [`jamal_profile.md`](jamal_profile.md) and the 125-line verified quote bank merged from the two per-book profiles. The bank stays LOCAL in `profiles/jamal_quote_bank.md` (gitignored, per SPEC) and its row numbers are the `#N`s cited below; this catalog ships, analysis quotes of unused and cut lines included. Characterization and every `original` line are ours. The book text never leaves the gitignored `extracts/`.

## Copyright posture (chotchki's locked call, SPEC.md)

Verbatim lines are short single-sentence-or-less utterances of one character, gathered under the non-commercial fan-work fair-use call; no scene, paragraph or exchange is reproduced anywhere. B.4 is LOCKED in full (2026-09-19, chotchki), so every `SOURCE: verbatim` row is a shipping decision, not a candidate flag. Two rulings have copyright or distribution consequences: the player is *Mr. Engineer* and every Dungeon Crawler Carl proper noun is out of the `Line` column except the two carve-outs Q5 names — *Velma* and *Gentlemen* (Q1/Q5, the ruling that created the third `SOURCE` tag below); and the one profanity line is CUT (Q2), so no shipping string contains profanity and the mod portal page needs no content note.

Every `verbatim` line has been machine-verified as an exact substring of its extract (103 distinct strings across 158 rows, plus every full quote in a `Note` cell and every `ORIGINAL:` quote behind an `adapted` row; all exact, zero fabrications). Re-verify before B.5 generates anything — a miss is a FABRICATION and gets deleted, not fixed:

```sh
cd character/extracts && grep -qF -- "<the line>" book7_jamal.txt   # or book8_jamal.txt
```

Trimmed lines are still verbatim because every trim is a CONTIGUOUS substring — a prefix, a suffix or a whole inner clause. Nothing is cut from the middle and stitched back together, which would produce a sentence he never said that still passes a careless eyeball check. The `Note` column carries the full quote wherever a trim exists, a promise the objective-defects pass had to make true: measured against the extracts rather than the `Note` cells, 44 rows were real trims and only 30 carried a marker: 14 were invisible to the regex below and 12 of those carried no full quote anywhere. The fourteen: nine trailing dialogue-tag commas noted in prose, `idle.02` with no trim note at all, `legs_break.09` / `flopping.08` dropping an address sentence AND a comma, and the `legs_break.16` / `flopping.29` pair, a suffix of a 208-character bk 8 sentence nothing in this file had ever quoted. All fourteen now carry a `TRIMMED (…) of` marker with the full quote behind it, or a pointer to the first occurrence that does.

A trim that needs its preceding clause to supply the SUBJECT is a bad trim (two passes running made that mistake). Contiguity keeps a trim honest about provenance; it says nothing about whether the result is a sentence. 45 rows carry an applied trim (a `Note` matching `TRIMMED (` or `TRIMMED, full quote` — every real trim, not just the marked ones), and 15 of them start mid-sentence in lower case (11 distinct strings; four of the fifteen are reuse rows). Most of those are fine: the book punctuates him in fragments, and a nominal fragment that anchors itself some other way (*a taste of my toasty emissions.*, *My apologies to you and your family*, *they are much too small for Jamal!*) reads correctly over a shark. The failure is narrower and much worse: a trim that leaves a BARE PRONOUN standing in for Jamal. His strongest tic is third person by his own name, and the profile states the negative outright — *not he, Jamal* — so a bubble opening on *he* points at nobody and inverts the tic at once.

Three fixes, in order (the third is not a defeat):

1. Re-cut to include the subject, if the name is on that side of the sentence (`jump.08`).
2. Make it `tail-only` behind a head that supplies the subject, if the name is not (`land.04`, `built.03`, `shore.06`, and `idle.21` for the same reason with a different missing word). This needs a pool whose event is not a swap: `repaired.04` carried `built.03`'s exact string and had to take option 3, because a chain scheduled by a swap is cancelled by it.
3. Cut the row. If neither works and the full quote won't fit a bubble, the material is not shippable. `idle.33` and `idle.38` went this way and the ocean beat with them, until E.2.3 found it a pool where option 2 works (`shore.05` → `shore.06`). `idle.22` went at the objective-defects pass for the adjacent reason: its defence was a chain the one-hop rule cannot express, so the mechanism justifying the row did not exist.

Re-trimming a verbatim row is WRITING a new line, not adjusting an old one, and it gets a fresh read against the profile. `idle.33`'s defect was manufactured by the pass that re-trimmed it to fix a different defect, and it passed that pass's review because contiguity still held. Contiguity is a provenance check, not a voice check.

`adapted` is the third provenance tag, for exactly one move: a real quote whose PERSON-REFERENCE we changed. Chotchki's B.4 Q1/Q5 ruling swaps the book's addressee for *Mr. Engineer*, and at `repaired.08` drops a third-party attribution with nobody left to point at. Such a line fits neither other tag — `original` claims we wrote it, `verbatim` lies to the fidelity checker. So `adapted` means the provenance is a real quote, the ONLY change is the person-reference and the untouched original sits in the `Note` cell behind the literal marker `ORIGINAL:` in straight double quotes. That marker is a machine contract: the checker greps it and validates the source line exactly as it validates a `verbatim` row, so an adapted row stays as traceable as the day it was a quote. 14 rows carry it, and the no-smoothing rule below still applies to every word around the swapped name.

Straight double quotes in a `Note` cell mean PROVENANCE and nothing else. They appear behind `ORIGINAL:` and `TRIMMED (…) of`, both naming a string that came out of a book; every other quoted string in a `Note` (a proposed trim, a row this file used to carry, a phrase under discussion) is in *italics*, as in the prose everywhere else. The voice-editor pass made this a rule: two `Note` cells put a PROPOSED trim in straight doubles (`flopping.38`, `jump_refused.19`), so a checker validating straight-quoted strings against the extracts would have been fed a string this file invented — it only passed because both trims happen to be substrings of their own verbatim rows. Fixed in place. The convention cannot be scoped to the table, because the checker reads the whole file and doesn't know which column it is in, so three PROSE strings doing the same thing were italicized at the objective-defects pass (*emit nothing*, *all three tiers*, *rewrite in register*). chotchki's own `- Comment:` lines are the one exemption: transcribed verbatim, never restyled.

Smoothing a quote is fabricating it. His double plurals (*sticky feets*), ellipses, em-dash interruption, curly apostrophes and trailing dialogue-tag commas are the signal, reproduced character for character. Where a line reads like a fragment (`Gentlemen,` / `I must give you an apology`), the book punctuates it that way.

The glyph is part of the string: every apostrophe in a `Line` cell is U+2019 (’), ours as well as his. Nine `original` rows shipped ASCII `'` beside thirteen book-derived rows with the curly one, so the generated locale would have carried two apostrophes for the same possessive — a diff nobody would read as a bug, and a typographic tell in-game. Fixed at the objective-defects pass: nine single-character edits, no ids touched. The prose here still uses whatever it was typed with; the rule covers `Line` cells because those are the strings that ship.

## How to read the table (the machine contract for B.5)

Every section is `## <event>`, then a `key:` line naming the Lua table key, a `split:` line, a `fires:` note, then ONE table with these twelve columns in this order:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ID` | stable, unique across the whole file. Becomes the locale key `jamaltron-line.<id>`. Never renumber an ID that has shipped |
| `Line` | the string, exactly as it should render. **Every row has one** — there is no longer any such thing as a pool entry with no string (see the channels section) |
| `CH` | `speech` \| `narration` — **which of the mod's two output channels this row goes to.** Get it wrong and the row speaks in the wrong voice; see the channels section immediately below |
| `SOURCE` | `verbatim` (exact substring of an extract), `adapted` (a real quote with only the person-reference changed; the untouched original sits in `Note` behind `ORIGINAL:`), or `original` (written by us in his voice). **Do not blur these** — the fidelity checker validates each tag differently |
| `BK` | `7` or `8` for verbatim, `-` for original |
| `W` | integer relative weight WITHIN this pool. Not a probability, not normalized — the picker sums the eligible rows and rolls |
| `TIER` | `quiet` \| `normal` \| `unbearable` |
| `SUB` | subpool, condition, advisory tag, or `-`/`any`. **What it MEANS is set by the section's `split:` line, never inferred from this column** — three incompatible meanings share this cell and only the `split:` line separates them; see below |
| `GATE` | `-` or `once_per_save` — a hard once-per-entity-per-save eligibility gate, orthogonal to `W` and `TIER`. 8 rows carry it; see the gate section |
| `CHAIN` | `-`, or `<row id>@<ticks>` on a SETUP row, or `tail` / `tail-only` on the follow-up it names. Newest column; the full contract is in the chaining section below |
| `GRP` | anti-repeat group: the ID of the FIRST row in this file carrying that exact `Line`. A unique string is its own group. **Computed, never hand-assigned.** D.5 keys anti-repeat on this, per ENTITY, across pools |
| `Note` | why the line is here, the full quote when trimmed, the `ORIGINAL:` quote when adapted, and the B.4 ruling where one applies |

### The two output channels — `CH` (chotchki's B.4 ruling, and it is structural)

The mod has TWO output channels. `CH` says which one a row belongs to, and a generator reads it right after `Line`.

- `speech` — Jamal talking, rendered as a speech bubble over the entity. Strict register: he puts himself in the SUBJECT slot (`Jamal is <verb>ing`), the honorific never drops and there is no irony and no wordplay outside his own malapropisms. 257 rows.
- `narration` — the GAME describing him: third person, ABOUT him rather than BY him. This is where the books' own narration voice legitimately lives — the dry aside, the footfall onomatopoeia, the flagged silence. Renders as something visibly not a bubble (floating text or a chat line; D.5 picks). 14 rows.

One column closed two findings and replaced six separate fixes. The rows a voice audit flagged as *off-voice narrator syntax* — `moving.03` *Clack, clack, clack go Jamal's feet.*, `jump.04` *Up goes Jamal!*, `land.08` — were never off-voice, they were in the WRONG CHANNEL. A fronted verb makes him the OBJECT of a sportscast, wrong in his mouth and exactly right in the narrator's, and the footfall onomatopoeia is the book's narrator looking at him from outside in the first place. Nothing needed cutting; three rows needed moving. The later voice audit found the ruling under-applied: two more rows sat in the wrong channel on the identical diagnosis (`flopping.23`, whose own Note called it a caption, and `moving.11`, using the narration's *whir* in the pool `moving.03` had just left), and `died.08` is mis-channelled for a different reason — a past-tense verdict on his whole self is an EPITAPH, and only a survivor utters one.

A channel move is not a rewrite, which the voice-editor pass had to fix next. `flopping.23` *Jamal waves his head about most miserably.* and `moving.11` *Jamal's legs are whirring most robustly.* were both `Jamal <verbs> most <adverb>`: *most* is his #2 lexical marker and third-person-by-name is his backbone, so once re-labelled `narration` the file shipped a narrator who talks exactly like Jamal. That is worse than the mis-routing, since it makes the channel distinction inaudible in the two pools where it renders most. Both were rewritten into a real narrator register — flat outside-in observation plus a dry gloss, the shape `low_health.01` and `land.08` already use, none of his markers. The narrator also gets to use a PRONOUN for Jamal, the cleanest audible split available: the name is his, the pronoun is the narrator's. `moving.11` now lands the book's own joke on top (he was built to be quiet as well as quick, and audibly is not). And `[SILENCE]` stops meaning *emit nothing* and becomes a narration note, the other half of the same ruling.

B.5 emits `CH` per row and D.5 renders on it. A generator that flattens the channel ships fourteen rows of Jamal saying things about himself in a voice he demonstrably does not have — the exact failure the audit caught, reintroduced by the tooling.

The `split:` line is the whole `SUB` contract, and it has exactly three values. `SUB` is deliberately overloaded (three incompatible meanings in one column), so a generator MUST read `split:` before the table:

- `none` — every row is in one pool. Any non-`-` `SUB` is an ADVISORY tag for B.4 (`sincere`, `meta`) and changes nothing about the roll. 9 of the 16 sections.
- `required — a | b` — a real fork. The mod knows which side fired before it rolls, rolls inside that side only and EVERY row picks a side. Two sections: `enter/exit` and `legs break`.
- `gated — <dimension>: <value> | <value> | ...` — conditional eligibility, the one that grew. The mod reports a condition at fire time; an `any` row is always eligible, a valued row only when the mod reports exactly that value, and a value the mod cannot report matches nothing, so its rows never roll. Five sections: `attacking` (`friendly`), `jump refused` (`reason`, B.6), `land` (`outcome`), `flopping` (`fork`), `shore` (`ground`, E.2.3).

`gated` generalized from one tag to a named dimension at the structural pass, and the old spelling still stands: `attacking` predates it and writes its always-eligible rows as `-` rather than `any`. In a `gated` section `-` and `any` mean the same thing; every section since spells it `any`, so a classification is visibly a DECISION rather than an omission. A generator needs one line for this.

Why `gated` and not `required` for the sections after `attacking`: a required fork makes every row pick a side, and in these sections most rows are condition-agnostic — a lament works whoever broke the legs, a grievance about the arc works whether or not a cliff was in the way. Forcing a side would halve each pool for no gain and push several tier cells under the four-string floor. `gated` keeps agnostic rows in both rolls and reserves the tag for rows that would LIE under the wrong condition, which is the only thing the split is for.

Three of the five `gated` dimensions are still under-reported by the mod, the safe direction: `attacking`'s `friendly` waits on the API (Q11), `jump refused`'s `reason` on E.2, `land`'s `outcome` on the E.3/E.4 reorder. `flopping`'s `fork` has been stored since D.5, and `shore`'s `ground` cannot lag, because the detector that fires the pool is what classifies the ground (E.2.2). Until each lands, the tagged rows sit out and the `any` rows carry the pool — quieter than designed, never wronger.

A generator that treats every non-`-` `SUB` as a fork silently breaks three pools: `idle`'s two weight-1 sincere lines become a coin flip against the other 30 rows, and `jump.13`, the single surviving `meta` row, becomes either half of all jumps or unreachable. That is the easiest way to get B.5 wrong. `SUB = sincere` is doubly advisory: those rows are gated by `GATE`, not `SUB`, so a generator that ignores `SUB` entirely on a `split: none` section is CORRECT, and one that forks on it is wrong twice. The tag survives on six rows and no longer on `jump_refused.20`, where B.6's real `reason` split took the column over; nothing was lost, because the eight gated rows are enumerated by ID below and the tag never drove anything.

Tiers are CUMULATIVE: `quiet` plays quiet only, `normal` plays quiet + normal, `unbearable` plays everything. So a `quiet` line is one good enough to survive a friend opting mostly out (the best ones), and `unbearable` is where the apology spam lives.

Tiers key off REGISTER, not just volume — the profile's most machinable finding and the likeliest to get flattened by accident. His escalation is INVERTED: formality is inversely proportional to severity, so routine events get the baroque politeness and the rare serious ones the plain short words. A tier system that only prunes frequency loses the joke.

Tokens (the generator substitutes; the locale file uses `__1__` style parameters):

- `{PLAYER}` — GONE, resolved 2026-09-19 (chotchki): the player is *Mr. Engineer*, baked as a literal into all 26 rows that carried the token. Factorio's engineer is canonically NAMELESS (Wube's own position), so there is no real name to use, and Jamal's honorific tic is about the FORM, not the name: *Mister* / *Mr.* / *Miss* + given name, never dropped, applied to literally everyone including a pet dinosaur. *Mr. Engineer* satisfies the form and is canonical Factorio. Baked rather than kept as a token because there is no per-player variance left to represent, and baking makes this file's authored character counts TRUE: B.5 substitutes and re-measures nothing. *Gentlemen* stays where the book used it (`legs_break.32`, `flopping.25`), since he addresses groups that way regardless of who is present.
- `{N}` — this entity's cumulative leg-break count. SHIPS (B.4 Q9). Both books count hardware out loud and book 7 counts it in protest; the mod already knows the number, so the gag improves every time it fires — the cheapest high-value feature in the character. 4 rows use it (`legs_break.17`, `legs_break.27`, `flopping.30`, `repaired.06`), topping out at 51 characters with a three-digit count (`repaired.06`).

  Storage cost, for E.6 to price: `{N}` needs `storage`-only per-entity state, which E.6 already requires for multiplayer sync — a table keyed by `unit_number` holding an integer, plus, in the same table, the `once_per_save` fired-set the `GATE` column needs. No mutable module-level state, no per-tick work, one entry per jamaltron. The entry is created lazily on the first break and removed on `on_entity_died` / `on_object_destroyed`, or it leaks one small record per destroyed entity for the life of the save.

`[SILENCE]` is GONE: the six silence rows are NARRATION rows with real strings (B.4 Q7). The old encoding (*the picker selects this row and emits nothing*) was wrong twice: a speech mod is structurally bad at silence, and an emitted absence is indistinguishable from a mod that failed to fire. In both books his going quiet is how the text says something is badly wrong, and both narrations FLAG it as the anomaly, so the mod flags it too, in the channel built for that.

chotchki's wording for the shape is *Jamal is silent... for once*. Each of the six carries its own wording rather than that string six times, because a silence at `died` and one at `low_health` are different moments, even though the books use his silence as the distress signal in both:

| Row | String | The moment |
|---|---|---|
| `attacking.03` | Jamal has stopped apologizing. Jamal is feeding. | bk 7's berserker frenzy switches the manners off entirely |
| `damaged.02` | Jamal makes a noise that is not a word. | damage is a NOISE in both books, never a sentence |
| `low_health.01` | Jamal has gone quiet. That is never a good sign. | the distress signal — deliberately TIED for the heaviest row in the file, with `jump_refused.08` since the B.6 reweight |
| `legs_break.18` | The legs come apart. Jamal says nothing at all. | bk 8's break is wordless; the event happens and he says nothing |
| `repaired.13` | Jamal is silent... for once. | the contented come-down, and the one pool that earns the dry *for once* |
| `died.01` | Jamal does not get up. | mercy. Nobody in two books is ever cruel to him, so no punchline |

All six are `SOURCE = original`, `BK = -`, `CH = narration`, at their previous weights and tiers. `died.01` reads *Jamal does not get up.* rather than *Jamal is quiet now.* since the voice audit (see `died`).

The prefix SHIPS, and D.5 applies it at render time. chotchki's Q7 wording specified the encoding literally — *Game Note: <Jamal is silent ... for once>* — and this file recorded the comment, dropped the prefix and deferred to D.5 without saying it had deferred anything, which is how a stated encoding goes missing. Decided and written down: every `narration` row renders as `Game Note: ` + the row's string, and the prefix lives in the RENDERER, not the `Line` column. One place instead of fourteen cells (fourteen chances to typo the same eleven characters); the length cap and the fidelity checker measure only the string we authored; and a wrapper D.5 owns changes once, in one file, when chotchki looks at real floating text in-game.

Two consequences:

- The ≤ 60 cap is a speech-BUBBLE constraint and narration does not render in a bubble, so the wrapper costs nothing against it. D.5's own width budget is the longest narration string plus the wrapper: 49 + 11 = 60 characters today (`flopping.23`, after the voice-editor rewrite; `low_health.01` and `attacking.03` tie one behind at 48).
- The angle brackets are read as chotchki's placeholder notation for the row's string, not literal characters. That is the one interpretation call in this ruling, flagged rather than buried and cheap to flip (one line here, one in D.5) if he wants them rendered.

Three of these rows assert an ONGOING silence, and a named silence the mod breaks inside a tick is a shrug. The profile's version of him going quiet is SUSTAINED — hours hanging off a ceiling saying nothing, a whole feeding frenzy, a wordless break — and naming the silence then talking straight over it is worse than never naming it, because the player learns the narration lies. D.5 MUST honour a suppression window on these three. Each has a boundary the mod already computes, so this is bookkeeping, not a new subsystem:

| Row | The window | The boundary the mod already has |
|---|---|---|
| `attacking.03` | the rest of the current combat burst | the `attacking` throttle — no row fires again until the gun has been quiet long enough to re-arm the pool |
| `legs_break.18` | the first `flopping` interval **and the `land` utterance in the break tick (R4)** | the apology timer's first tick is skipped, which is also bk 8's actual beat: the break, and only then the flopping. The break tick is the other half: `land` resolves in it, and five `any` rows there are eligible on a break |
| `low_health.01` | 15 s of `speech` on that entity (suggested — A.7 can tune it against a real bubble) | one timestamp per entity, in the same `storage` record as `{N}` and the `once_per_save` fired-set |

Scope: the window is per ENTITY, suppresses the `speech` channel only (the narrator is not him, so narration still fires) and SUPPRESSES rather than re-rolls — emitting a second-choice line is the exact failure this ruling prevents. It gates BEFORE the roll, which keeps the paragraph after next true: the picker still has no emit-nothing branch, because a suppressed event never reaches it. `damaged.02`, `repaired.13` and `died.01` need no window: a noise is not a silence, a wreck has nothing left to contradict it, and the come-down at `repaired.13` is the only one that could earn a window later, if in-game the bubble after it reads as the mod stepping on its own beat.

And `died` is EXEMPT from every window (R3 below), because the two rules above contradict each other at exactly one event. *Narration still fires* and *a suppressed event never reaches the picker at all* cannot both hold for a gate that runs before the roll, since the gate does not know which channel the roll would pick. `low_health.01`'s 15 s window opens seconds before most deaths (it fires when health crosses 0.15), so an event-level window can swallow the whole `died` event, narration and all, and the mod goes silent at the one moment it has exactly one thing to say. So the window is evaluated per CHANNEL, never on the event, and `died` ignores it outright. A death is not the mod talking over its named silence; it is what the silence warned about.

This SIMPLIFIES the Lua shape B.5 emits: no pool entry lacks a string, so the generator has no absent-string branch and D.5's picker has no *emit nothing* path to get wrong. The only new conditional is which channel to render in.

Line length: hard target ≤ 60 characters, because it renders in a Factorio speech bubble over a walking vehicle. Two shipped lines sit slightly over (61 and 64), flagged in place with a trim offered; everything else fits. Median line length is 38 characters, which matches him: his median usable utterance across two books is 5–9 words.

## Anti-repeat — the `GRP` contract (B.4 Q13, locked)

Anti-repeat keys on `GRP`, per ENTITY, ACROSS pools — not on `ID`, not per pool. 210 distinct strings fill 271 rows and 48 of those strings live in more than one pool, occupying 109 rows, so per-pool suppression is blind exactly where it matters: `legs break` and `flopping` fire back to back (the break, then the apology timer) and share 15 strings. A per-pool suppressor cannot see that `flopping` is about to re-say the break line the player read two seconds ago, at the mod's centerpiece moment. Same shape, smaller, for `built` ∩ `repaired` (5 shared), and *Jamal must protest!* / *Nothing is wrong.* each span four pools.

`GRP` is the ID of the FIRST row in this file carrying that exact `Line`; a row with a unique string is its own group. It is COMPUTED, never hand-assigned — hand-assignment is how one sentence lands in two groups and the mechanism silently stops working while still looking correct in review.

The locale file is NOT deduplicated: one key per `ID`, always. A few hundred bytes buys a total ID→key mapping and leaves a reused line free to diverge per pool later. `GRP` is for the picker, `ID` for the locale; conflating them gets it wrong in both directions at once.

One canonical exception, recorded as an explicit allow-list: `idle.01` → `idle.02` is the same observation said once and repeated later verbatim with *indeed* bolted on. He is the only character in the books who quotes himself, and a system allowed to repeat that one succession reproduces a gag he performs on himself. Two notes: the pair is two different strings and so two different groups, so a strictly group-keyed suppressor already permits it — the allow-list exists so a later near-duplicate heuristic cannot silently kill the gag. And it is an ORDERED pair, not symmetric; the callback only works in that direction.

`idle.01` → `idle.02` does NOT become a `CHAIN`, and chaining is the reason. In the book the callback lands CHAPTERS later, and the joke is that he is still pleased with a line nobody else remembers; a chain fires the second beat on a two-second timer, turning a surprise callback into a stutter. Permitted-but-not-scheduled is the correct encoding: the allow-list stays, and both rows' `CHAIN` cells stay `-`.

Chain follow-ups are EXEMPT from everything in this section. A `tail` delivered by its head does not consult `GRP`, respect the suppression window or touch the cooldown — the pair is one utterance in two beats, and only the HEAD is a new utterance. A follow-up the picker rolls INDEPENDENTLY (a `tail`, never a `tail-only`) is an ordinary pick with the ordinary treatment.

## Line chaining — the `CHAIN` contract (B.4 answer #14, semantics LOCKED in SPEC.md)

Some rows only parse after a partner line, and `GRP` anti-repeat actively works against delivering one. *I have, I mean.* corrects a *Jamal* that has to have been said first. *How many lemurs?* is a follow-up to a question. *Jamal is giving you all of them.* has no antecedent for *them*. The catalog carried the problem from B.3 with no mechanism; B.7 adds the column, and every semantic below is chotchki's, transcribed from SPEC.md rather than invented here.

B.7 named nine rows and the count was wrong in both directions — the kind of error a list of ids hides. `flopping.21` is a tenth orphan with the identical defect, missed because it is a REUSED string: its `Note` lived at `legs_break.12` (since cut), so the row carrying the problem carried no note about it. And `jump.08` is not an orphan this mechanism can fix, because its partner is one EVENT away, not one delay away. Ten chains ship, and they are neither the nine the plan listed nor the nine that shipped at B.7: the objective-defects pass cut `legs_break.05` → `legs_break.12` as unreachable and wired `built.05` → `built.03` for a bare pronoun the trim sweep had missed (one for one, still nine), and E.2.3 added the tenth, `shore.05` → `shore.06`.

The column grammar, which is the whole machine contract:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `-` | not part of any chain. 251 rows |
| `<row id>@<ticks>` | **HEAD.** When this row fires, schedule that row on the same entity after that many ticks. The target is always in the SAME pool |
| `tail` | **FOLLOW-UP that also stands alone.** A head names it; the picker may also roll it normally |
| `tail-only` | **FOLLOW-UP that does not stand alone.** The picker MUST exclude it from every ordinary roll. Miss this and the punchline fires by itself, which is worse than not having the pair at all |

One hop only, so a `tail` is never also a head and the grammar cannot express a three-beat gag. A walking vehicle does not hold an audience for three beats.

One hop only also means a row cannot be defended by a beat it can never arrive behind, which cost `idle.22` its place. Its dangling *It* was justified in its `Note` by `idle.20` → `idle.21` supplying the preceding beats — but `idle.21` is `tail-only`, not a head and unable to become one, so nothing can be scheduled after it. A row whose sense depends on being the THIRD beat of a run is unshippable under this contract, not merely unwired: the rescuing mechanism is the one thing the grammar forbids.

The rest of the semantics, in force verbatim from SPEC.md: the follow-up is EXEMPT from anti-repeat, the `GRP` suppression window and the say-cooldown — those gate new utterances, and the punchline is the second half of the utterance already in flight. It INHERITS the head's tier, so a `normal` follow-up behind a `quiet` head plays for a `quiet` player and the `TIER` cell on a `tail-only` row is advisory only; gating it at its own tier would fire setups whose punchline is silently dropped. It is CANCELLED if the entity dies, is swapped (D.4, including the beached swap) or is mined before it fires — a punchline delivered by a corpse is a bug. Pending chains live in the same per-entity `storage` table as `{N}`, the `once_per_save` fired-set and the `flopping` fork.

The delay is COMPUTED, not taste: `3.5 ticks per character of the HEAD's string, rounded to the nearest 30, floor 60` — long enough to read the first bubble, short enough that the two beats are one utterance. B.5 recomputes it from the head rather than copying the number, the same way `GRP` is computed; a hand-tuned delay is a number nobody can re-derive after the head gets re-trimmed (`idle.20` was re-trimmed in this very pass, and its delay moved with it).

### Reachability — the rule the locked semantics imply

A chain is only real if nothing between setup and payoff cancels it, and one of B.7's nine failed that from the day it was written. The cancel rule and the chain rule were locked separately in the same B.4 answer, and they collide at exactly one kind of event: `legs_break.05` → `legs_break.12` was scheduled by the event that IS the swap to `jamaltron-beached`, and a swap cancels pending follow-ups. No delay could deliver that punchline, so the head's `CHAIN` cell is cleared and `legs_break.12` is cut — the verdict `attacking.14` got for being unreachable in its own pool. Nothing is lost: the pair is intact at `flopping.11` → `flopping.21`, one pool later, where he is already beached.

The generalized rule, which B.5 checks mechanically: no chain may be scheduled by an event that is ITSELF a swap. Two pools fire at a swap — `legs break` (walking → beached) and `repaired` (beached → walking) — and in both the utterance and the swap land in the same tick, so whether the follow-up survives depends on which side of the swap D.5 schedules it. This catalog cannot assert that, so it doesn't: `repaired` gets no chain either, which is why `repaired.04` was CUT rather than made `tail-only` behind `repaired.02` the way `built.03` sits behind `built.05`. Its string survives at `built.03`, chained, in a pool whose event is a creation, not a transition.

### The fork bug, and the ORDER that fixes it

The pool used to fire BEFORE the break roll, so it could only guess. `land.01` *Jamal has landed. Nothing came apart!* (weight 8) and `land.06` *The legs held! Top quality after all.* (weight 5) assert an outcome the mod had not rolled — 13 of the 26 weight at `quiet`, in a pool with four strings there. On the landing that snaps his legs, a `quiet` player had a coin-flip chance of being told the legs held and then watching them come apart: the mod contradicting itself inside one second, at the moment the whole mod exists to deliver.

The fix is the SEQUENCE, and the sub is a belt on top of it. Reordering E.3/E.4 so the break roll resolves first is the actual repair; tagging the rows means a future refactor that quietly restores the old order still cannot pick a line that lies. The requirement is in the header (*Requirements the catalog cannot enforce*, R1), because no column can enforce ordering.

## flopping

**key:** `flopping`
**split:** gated — `fork`: `any` | `self` | `external`, **INHERITED from the `legs_break` event that started the flop, not re-decided per tick.** `any` rows always roll; `self` and `external` rows roll only under the fork the break carried
**fires:** repeating timer while he is `jamaltron-beached` (PLAN E.4), interval scaled by verbosity tier. The comedic centerpiece of the WHOLE mod and the only pool designed to be heard dozens of times in a row.

The punishment is not immobility, it is INDIGNITY. Freshly wrecked he is slow and pitiful in both books, complains loudly and continuously while flopping, and keeps addressing the group as if still mid-conversation. The apology spam IS the penalty, so this is the biggest pool in the file and the tier split does real work: at `quiet` he is sympathetic, at `unbearable` he is a war crime. One row moved channel at the voice audit and was then rewritten at the voice-editor pass: `flopping.23` watches his head-waving from outside, which is the narrator's job and not his (he reports affect as a meter reading, never as observed body language) — but the string it moved with, *Jamal waves his head about most miserably.*, was `Jamal <verbs> most <adverb>`, still in his voice. A narrator who talks like Jamal makes the channel inaudible, so the string went too.

### The fork this pool used to erase (D.5 requirement R2)

`legs_break` splits `self` | `external`, and this pool used to erase the distinction within a second. The external fork is defined by what he does NOT do — *He does not apologize at all* — and `legs_break.25` *Jamal did NOT hop! Jamal wishes this noted.* was promoted to `quiet` for one reason: so a player who asked for the least Jamal can still tell which break fired. Then the apology timer starts, and `flopping` rolled one undifferentiated pool in which 37.6% of the ROLLABLE weight (67 of 178, counted rather than eyeballed) is the apology register. At that share the chance of three intervals without one is 0.624³ = 24%, so the fork the mod just spent a `quiet` row announcing is gone within three apology intervals 76% of the time — the default outcome, not a rare collision. (The figure has been wrong three times and the conclusion never moved: 45%, 88 of 194, until the voice-editor pass dropped `flopping.21` — a `tail-only` row the picker never rolls — from the denominator and cut `flopping.05` and `flopping.31`; then 43%, 76 of 178, until the objective-defects pass noticed 76 was the weight of the `self` TAG, not the apology register. This section's own classification rule puts `.18` (the hop-defence) and `.27` (the hardware denial) on `self` for reasons that are explicitly not apology, and dropping their 9 weight gives 67. The number is the argument for R2, and an argument citing the wrong measurement is one recount from being dismissed.)

So `flopping` carries the same fork, and D.5 must roll it with the fork the break event carried. The fork belongs to the BREAK, not the tick: stamped on the entity at break time, read on every apology tick, cleared at `repaired`. It rides in the same per-entity `storage` record as `{N}`, the `once_per_save` fired-set and the pending chains — one more field, no new machinery. Defaulting rule, inherited from `legs_break` unchanged: when fault is ambiguous, default to `self` and defend the hardware.

The classification rule is mechanical on purpose, so a later reader can check it rather than trust it:

- `self` (16 rows) — every row in the APOLOGY register: it says *sorry* / *sorries* / *apology* / *apologizes* / *shame and regret*, or is one of the apology SHAPES the profile names (the self-narrated *blunder*, the intent disclaimer at `.38`, the take-the-blame confession at `.34`). No apology-register row is eligible on `external`, and that single sentence is the whole mechanism. `.18` *I don't see why Jamal shouldn't be able to hop.* is here for the other reason: on an external break he never hopped, so defending the hop is a non sequitur. `.27` *Nothing is wrong.* follows its own `legs_break` assignment (`legs_break.19`, `self`).
- `external` (4 rows) — the grievance register: `.06` *Top quality!*, `.14` *And I was led to believe they were top quality.*, `.30` *Set {N} was not top quality either* and `.26` *Jamal must protest!*. The `self` fork forbids a quality complaint, ever, so these four are exactly the rows that must not leak across.
- `any` (18 rows) — misery, status readouts, being stuck, asking to be turned over, the rescue request, the narration row. He does these on both forks, and splitting them would halve the pool for nothing. `.17` *they are much too small for Jamal!* stays `any` deliberately: it complains about the SPARES on offer, not the set that broke, and book 7 has him make it on a break that was his own fault.

Both forks clear the floor, the number that decides whether a split is affordable: `self` runs 6 / 24 / 33 distinct strings by tier, `external` 4 / 17 / 22. External at `quiet` sits exactly ON the floor of four — *I do fear I require assistance*, *Such woe!*, *Jamal is stuck!*, *Top quality!* — and it is the right four: no apology in any of them, and the one grievance row is the running gag. It is also the tightest spot in the file, so if anything here ever needs a new line, it is that cell.

Two B.4 cuts, and the pool was renumbered (old `.38`–`.41` are now `.37`–`.40`):

- The swear is gone (old `flopping.42`, the same string as old `legs_break.33`); pool tail, so it cost no ids. The maximum-misery beat now falls to `flopping.38` *It is not Jamal's intention to add any hair-pulling distress.* — not the heaviest row in the pool (the metronome apologies outweigh it several times over) but the DEEPEST one left: it denies INTENT rather than fault, which prices a baseline of distress from him in and claims only the surplus. Bleaker than the swear, and it was already shipping.
- The old `flopping.37` *Jamal is not a burden. Jamal is merely horizontal.* was DROPPED, not rewritten. The best-written original in the file and the furthest out of register: the wit is the WRITER's, about his condition, and he does not do wit about himself. Everything it carried (bother-aversion stated flatly, the thing that keeps the player annoyed rather than resentful) is already carried in register by `flopping.37` (formerly `.38`) *Jamal is sorry to be a bother. Jamal is a bother.*, where the joke is that he agrees with the accusation. A replacement would have been filler; fewer originals beats flat ones.

Two more rows were CUT at the voice-editor pass, ids retired, no replacement written:

## jump refused

**key:** `jump_refused`
