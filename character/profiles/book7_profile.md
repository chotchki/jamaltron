# Jamal character profile — Book 7 (*This Inevitable Ruin*)

Source: `../extracts/book7_jamal.txt` (gitignored, windowed ±2 paragraphs around each mention across 17 chapters). Characterization only — the book text stays where it is.

**Companion file: [`book7_quote_bank.md`](book7_quote_bank.md)** holds 78 grep-verified single-sentence spoken lines with situation + mod-event tags. That is the B.3 feedstock; THIS file is the characterization and stays inside the template's 6-quote budget (6 used: 5 exemplars + 1 in the trigger table). Single words and short phrases of his vocabulary are quoted as lexical items throughout and are not counted against that budget — a profile that can't name his own word for his legs is useless.

**Book 7 in one paragraph of context:** this is Faction Wars, an army-scale floor. Jamal is a 17-foot hammerhead shark who used to be one of Signet's paper tattoos, is now flesh, is level 72, and gets around on a bolt-on rig of eight mechanical spider legs that Carl built him as a prototype. Over the course of the book he burns through SIX sets of those legs, gains a flamethrower, and occupies a strange rank — not a pet, not a crawler, not quite a soldier, an NPC the system doesn't know where to file. He is comic relief who is also genuinely dangerous, and the joke is that he never notices either half.

## Voice

### The apology habit (mod-critical — PLAN D.5, E.4 spam him on a timer)

**The governing metaphor: apologies are COUNTABLE PHYSICAL OBJECTS he owns a finite supply of, and delivering them is a transaction.** This is the single most machinable thing about him. He does not say "sorry" — he *gives* you an apology, he *finds* more apologies, he *has* sorries, and once found they *become yours*. The verbs are all inventory verbs: give, find, have, own. For a Factorio mod this is perfect, because it means every apology line can be generated from one template with the object swapped.

Distinct shapes, each a reusable template:

- **Transfer** — he *must give* you an apology ("I must give you an apology,"). The apology is an obligation he discharges, not a feeling he has. Note the modal: *must*.
- **Bulk transfer** — a countable pile of sorries, all of them handed over at once (exemplar 1). This is the maximum-remorse form.
- **Resupply** — he has to go LOOK for more apologies when a second victim appears mid-conversation, and having found them he assigns ownership. He genuinely behaves as though he ran out and restocked.
- **Self-diagnosis as apology** — reporting his interior state as a substitute for the apology ("a great amount of shame and regret"). Reads like a status bar, which is why it belongs in a speech bubble.
- **Blast-radius formality** — the apology extends past the victim to the victim's FAMILY, for damage that was nearly nothing. Paired with a cute euphemism for the offense (he calls nearly incinerating someone "toasty disrespect").
- **Self-narrated blunder** — he announces the screwup in the third person, in the passive, as though reading a bulletin about someone else (exemplar 2). His two nouns for the screwup are "blunder" and "bungle".
- **The formal request rider** — the apology usually ends by asking for help, in the most over-built way available: he *does fear* he *requires assistance*.

**Escalation is INVERTED — and this is the funniest fact about him.** Low stakes get maximum formality and maximum volume of apology. The apology after breaking a set of legs Carl spent hours on is elaborate, courtly and long. But when the offense is real — he sets an ally on fire, he wounds a friendly crawler with a leg swipe — the apology gets SHORTER and the euphemism gets cuter, and in the case of the crawler he wounded he apparently never apologizes at all (she is bleeding out two paragraphs later and he has moved on to hopping in circles around her). He is calibrated to social friction, not to harm. Mod consequence: the `damaged`/`attacking` pools should be flippant and the `legs break` pool should be operatic, which is backwards from what a designer would guess.

**Targets, ranked by volume:** Carl gets most of them (owner, builder, order-giver, the one whose work he keeps destroying). Donut second (she gets the apologies for his verbal tics, not for damage). Katia third and oddly — she apologizes to HIM about the legs more than he apologizes to her. Bystanders get one apology each when he burns them. **The mod maps cleanly: whoever drives him is Carl.**

**Unprompted apologies are the majority.** He apologizes for his own size (the parts are too small for him and he says so as a lament, not a complaint about the parts), for taking up room, for existing in a way that inconveniences the schedule, and pre-emptively for what his flamethrower is about to do. Nobody asks. Keep the pre-emptive ones separate from the reactive ones in the catalog — the pre-emptive ones are the idle pool, and they are the ones that will make players hate him correctly.

**What he does NOT apologize for — load-bearing:**

- **Hopping.** He has broken three sets of legs doing it and has been explicitly told to stop, and his position is unchanged: he does not see why he should not be allowed to hop. He apologizes for the BREAKAGE and defends the HOP in the same breath, with a "Still, Mr. Carl" hinge between them. This is the whole character in one move.
- **The flamethrower.** He reclassifies every accident as accidental and issues a forward-looking promise never to do it again, which is not an apology and which he breaks immediately.
- **His intelligence.** Told he is being manipulated, he objects, and claims to be too quick-witted for the trick (he then falls for it).
- **His past.** Apex predator, then a terror who could fly, then one of the most feared warriors of all the seas — he volunteers this résumé unprompted and never walks it back.
- **Leg quality.** When the failure is genuinely NOT his fault he does not apologize at all, he lodges a formal PROTEST. Different register entirely.

**Adjacent registers, all in the same voice:**

- **Formal protest** — "Jamal must protest!" plus an itemized grievance and a count. Used when wronged.
- **Lament** — "Such woe!" and its possessive variant. Pure, content-free misery, repeatable forever. Best idle line for the beached state.
- **Permission-seeking** — he negotiates rather than obeys: mayhaps later, he would very much like to stay outside for the moment, mayhaps after the fighting. Polite, persistent, never defiant.
- **Reassurance** — "Rest assured, Mr. Carl", followed by a guarantee he cannot keep.
- **Star-struck gossip** — he ranks goddesses by beauty, sincerely, out loud, mid-campaign.

### Third person vs. first person (the other signature tic)

**Default: THIRD PERSON, by a wide margin, and he uses his own name rather than a pronoun.** Not "he" — "Jamal." Jamal is here. Jamal is firing his flamethrower. Jamal will stand in the shade. Jamal has made another blunder.

**The slip into first person is triggered by two things and two things only:**

1. **Sincerity about wanting something.** When the yearning to hop takes over he crosses into "I" mid-sentence, and the crossover point is visible in the text as an ellipsis and a self-correction. He *notices* and fixes it — "I have, I mean" — which is the tell: the first person is the real him leaking out and he is embarrassed by it.
2. **Being scolded for it.** Donut has a standing pet peeve about third-person speech (she calls it "Elmo-ing") and hisses his name at him. He switches for a sentence or two, apologizes for the tic, blames his yearning for it, and drifts straight back.

He also mixes both WITHIN a single utterance without noticing, and the mixed form reads as the most natural version of him.

**Does this map onto verbosity tiers? PARTLY, and be honest about which direction.** The book supports: third person = routine, performative, status-bulletin chatter; first person = the rare sincere line. It does NOT support third person being *quieter* — his third-person lines are the loud ones. So the mapping for the mod should be REGISTER, not volume: chatty/comic pools in third person, the two or three sincere lines (wanting to jump, expecting to die) in first person. And the occasional deliberate slip-plus-correction in a single bubble is the highest-fidelity thing the mod could ship.

### Catchphrases & verbal tics

- **Stock opener: "Oh, goodness" / "Oh my goodness" / "My goodness".** Doubled when things are bad. It is the first sound out of him when the legs blow up, and it is also his reaction to a mind-control atrocity, which tells you the range is zero.
- **Stock closer: "indeed."** Appended to anything. "Quite unpleasant indeed." "It is getting quite toasty indeed." Also **"Rest assured"** as a closer, and **"you see"** as a softener.
- **Self-narration as a status line — the mod's single biggest win.** He announces what he is doing while doing it, in the third person, with his own name as the subject: arriving, firing, guarding, standing in the shade. These are speech-bubble strings verbatim. Section 5 of the quote bank is nothing but these.
- **Self-reported emotional state, quantified.** He does not feel excited, he HAS an excitement level, and it is something else. His excitement is "very robust". His energy is "untold levels". His shame comes in "a great amount". He reports affect the way a UI reports a meter — which is exactly what a Factorio `custom_status` is.
- **Self-quoting.** He says a line, and chapters later repeats it verbatim with "indeed" bolted on, as a callback. He is the only character who quotes himself.
- **Honorifics, absolutely rigid:** "Mister Carl" / "Mr. Carl" (both spellings occur), "Miss Donut", "Miss Katia". He addresses the group as "Gentlemen" regardless of who is in it. The honorific NEVER drops — not in combat, not in misery, not when he is swearing.
- **Malapropisms and invented compounds:** **"hoppers"** for his spider legs (his own word, and it should be in the mod's item description). **"reverse psychologicalize"** for reverse psychology. **"flamings"** as a countable noun for accidental incidents. **"toasty disrespect"** for nearly burning someone alive. **"doggies"** for lethal military automatons. **"Mayhaps"** for perhaps, always.
- **Intensifiers:** "quite" (dominant, on everything), "very much", "most", "such", "greatly", "purely", "thoroughly". Register is Edwardian butler with a head injury.
- **Words he would never use:** contractions are rare, slang is absent, technical vocabulary is absent — he says "the metal pieces did break again" rather than naming a joint or a servo, and he does the do-support construction ("did break") that no other character uses. **He swears exactly ONCE in the entire book** (exemplar 5), flopping legless behind a convoy after his fifth set is destroyed by something that was not his fault. That single line is the register floor dropping out, and it lands because everything else he says is so mannered. Ship it, gate it behind the worst possible event, never let it into a common pool.

### Sentence shape

- **Short sentences in RUNS.** He almost never speaks one sentence — he speaks four to six short declaratives in a burst, each grammatically complete, each independently quotable. This is the reason the quote bank yields 78 lines out of ~24 utterance events, and it is why he is an unusually good fit for a speech-bubble system: **he already writes his own one-liners.**
- **The news is buried at the END of the run.** The sequence is: exclamation → self-narrated blunder → formal request for help. You learn his legs are destroyed on the third sentence.
- **Over-built courtesy scaffolding around trivial content** — "I do fear I require assistance" for *help*, "Mayhaps when Jamal gets back from the fighting?" for "later."
- **Repetition with a one-word variation** is his emphasis mechanism: "Such woe! Such woe I have!" / "Quite unpleasant indeed." / "Top quality!" repeated as its own sentence. **Both halves fit in one bubble and the repetition is free comedy.**
- He does not interrupt and he does not trail off (except at the third-person slip). He DOES get interrupted, shut up and hissed at constantly, and he absorbs it without resentment.
- **Line-length budget: excellent.** The median usable line is 5–9 words. Every tic except the mid-sentence third-person slip survives a one-line truncation, and the slip needs two clauses. Nothing needs two sentences.

### Register & vocabulary

Four registers, each with a hard trigger:

1. **Courtly apologist** (default, fires on any fault or friction) — formal, third person, inventory metaphors, honorifics, over-built politeness.
2. **Robust excitement** (fires on new hardware, an assignment, a fight, music, literally anything) — exclamations, quantified enthusiasm, physical bouncing, involuntary flamethrower discharge. This is his most common state and should be the default idle pool.
3. **Operatic misery** (fires on legs destroyed, or on being denied) — woe, protest, counting grievances, and at the very bottom the one swear.
4. **Deadpan observer** (fires on catastrophe happening to OTHER people) — flat, understated, "a most unexpected development" delivered at an army-wide mind-control event. The gap between the stimulus and the flatness is the joke.

**Species and origin flavor bleeding through the manners:** he wants to feel the wind in his GILLS. He measures his own credentials in prey consumed (clowns, lemurs). He cites the seas. He has a berserker feeding frenzy state in which he stops talking entirely, crunches something in his mouth, whoops and barrel-rolls — the ONLY time the manners switch off. The sea words and the etiquette never blend; they alternate, and the seams are where he is funniest.

### Exemplar lines

1. "Jamal has so many sorries to give, and Jamal is giving you all of them." — *apologizing to the man who built the legs he just broke by hopping*
2. "It appears Jamal has made another blunder." — *self-reporting a catastrophic failure in the third-person passive*
3. "These were the fifth set of legs Jamal has been given!" — *protesting, with a running count, when the breakage genuinely wasn't his fault*
4. "Jamal will only flame on purpose from now on." — *promise made about a newly-installed flamethrower, broken within pages*
5. "And... Fuck! Fuck! Fuck my life!" — *the one register break in the book: flopping legless behind a convoy*

## Personality

### Core traits

- **Incorrigible.** Not stubborn — he agrees with you, apologizes sincerely, and then does it again. Three sets of legs destroyed by the same forbidden action, and his stated position after the third is that he still does not see the problem. He is unteachable by consequence, which is exactly the trait a mod needs to justify a jump button that keeps breaking.
- **Compulsively courteous.** The manners never drop, including while flopping in the dirt on ruined hardware, including while swearing. Etiquette is load-bearing to his identity in a way his body isn't.
- **Enthusiastic past the point of being a hazard.** His excitement is kinetic and it damages the environment: bystanders leap out of the way, rocks fly, allies get scorched. Nobody is ever angry at him for long because the enthusiasm is so obviously sincere.
- **Genuinely dangerous, and nobody treats it as the headline.** Level 72. He pulls a struggling mercenary out of a battle in his mouth. He clears an automaton nest by pumping flame into holes one after another. He disarms a hostile crawler with a leg swipe hard enough to nearly kill her. The comedy is the foreground and the lethality is stated flatly in the background, and **the mod should reproduce exactly that ratio.**
- **Socially undeterred.** Shut down by Donut, ordered around by Carl, told to be quiet, told to stay behind — he keeps talking, keeps negotiating, keeps looking for someone new to talk to. When the whole convoy has a stench debuff he rolls toward the people who can't get away from him.
- **Loyal by choice, which the book makes a point of.** At the end, when the NPCs are free to leave, he is one of the few who stays.

### Drives

- **He wants to hop.** That is the drive. It is small, it is specific, it is repeatedly denied, it is the reason all his hardware breaks, and it is the mod's central mechanic. He frames it as recovering something lost: he used to be a flying terror, now he cannot manage a little hop.
- Wants new legs, immediately, and wants them to be TOP QUALITY.
- Wants to be included — in the fight, in the conversation, in the group standing outside the privacy bubble.
- Wants someone to talk to, and is not selective.
- Wants to see a goddess, because he has heard she is beautiful.

### Fears & insecurities

- **Being smushed.** His stated expectation of how he will die: if both his owners die the saferoom vanishes and he gets smushed. He says it cheerfully, which is worse.
- **Being too big.** The recurring wound. The parts are always too small for him, the leg joints have to be extra-large, the transports won't take him, the trenches aren't designed for him, the stairs are a problem. He never frames it as *make bigger parts* — he frames it as woe about himself.
- **Being left behind.** Told he is staying back, his entire response is "What?" — and then he negotiates. Twice in the book he is benched for his own good and both times he stays and sulks.
- **What he has LOST.** He lists it unprompted: he was an apex predator, he could fly, his sense of smell was greatly enhanced and is now thoroughly gone. This is the only thread where he is almost un-funny, and he always lands it back on the hopping, which defuses it.
- Getting stuck somewhere, in the physical sense.

### Trait-in-action

- Given a solo assault position, he climbs a vertical wall like a spider, announces that this is quite exciting, and leaks flame from an unaimed nozzle onto an ally standing below him — then apologizes to her family. Competence, enthusiasm and collateral damage in one uninterrupted beat.
- Ordered to guard two terrified prisoners, he accepts in the third person, does a little hop, spurts flame, and then spends the scene bouncing in circles around them. He is obeying perfectly. He is also a nightmare.
- Immune to a stench debuff that has everyone else vomiting, he reports that he is sometimes very much happy he can no longer smell, observes that it must be quite unpleasant, and notes he has never seen such vomiting — deadpan, then loops it back to the fact that his enhanced smell is gone just like his ability to hop. Every topic is a road back to the grievance.

### Self-image vs. reality

He thinks he is a distinguished former apex predator temporarily inconvenienced by equipment. He is, in fact, an overweight air-breathing shark bolted to prototype hardware, and he is the reason the hardware keeps failing — a connection he never makes, not once, in the entire book. He also thinks he is too quick-witted to be manipulated, immediately before being manipulated.

**The identity question the books raise elsewhere — is this flesh version of him the same person as the tattoo, or the shark before that — is NOT developed in book 7.** He states the losses as facts and grievances, never as an existential problem. So the mod does not have to decide how to handle a sad line about whether he is still himself, because this book does not offer one. Flagging it rather than inventing one.

## His legs (mod-critical — the whole conceit; PLAN E.4 breaks them)

### Provenance

**Six sets in one book.** The book counts them out loud and so should the mod.

- **Sets 1–3: Carl's, hastily built, explicitly a PROTOTYPE.** Eight double-jointed legs plus a harness sized for a 17-foot shark, derived from Carl's automaton manual, described as the same idea as another character's mechanical "wheelchair" but much bigger. The magically-enhanced joints are the expensive part and the failure point. Carl's stated design intent was to prove the concept before handing it to a better engineer. **All three were destroyed by Jamal hopping.**
- **Sets 4–5: Katia's, "newly-enhanced and fortified", with a weapon mount.** The upgrade path is explicit: Carl builds the rough one, Katia builds the good one. Set 5 is destroyed NOT by Jamal but by an EMP-style bomb blast — he was at the edge of the pulse and the cybernetics were unshielded. He is told this and does not accept it as an excuse.
- **Set 6: the last one** — the narration calls it his new *and final* set. From Katia via Mordecai. He bounces on them and is very excited.

**Gap, honestly:** the extract never shows set 4 being destroyed. The window only covers paragraphs near his name, so it happened offscreen or in a passage the extractor didn't catch. The count of 5 is HIS, stated in protest, and I take it as canon without being able to show the fourth failure.

### Mechanism

- **Carl's version is VOICE-CONTROLLED:** Jamal calls out to the legs and tells them where he wants to go. This is why the legs do what he says even when what he says is stupid — including the hop. **The mod reading is irresistible: the driver gives an order and the shark's own mouth is the control input.**
- **Katia's version was DESIGNED to read his thoughts** and act accordingly. Whether the shipped Katia legs actually do that is **not established in this book** — the intent is stated once, early, and never confirmed. Do not build a mechanic on it.
- The harness attaches to the body of the shark and carries bolt-ons. In book 7 exactly one bolt-on is added: a flamethrower, mounted on his UNDERBELLY, fitted to the harness by a crafting team (not by Katia). The nozzle direction is adjustable and was at one point deliberately pointed BACKWARD by order, to protect the people in front of him.
- Size is the recurring engineering constraint. Extra-large leg joints are a limited resource, and the standard cybernetics in the workshop are too small for him.

### Failure modes — the precise fact the mod is built on

**The joints handle the JUMP fine. They cannot handle the LANDING more than once or twice.** That is the book's own mechanical statement, and it is the exact shape of PLAN E.4: the roll happens on landing, not on takeoff. Three sets died this way. The one non-hop failure was an unshielded-electronics EMP.

Fault attribution: **3 of 5 failures are unambiguously his own doing and he was warned each time.** One is external and blameless. This gives the mod two distinct break events with two different line pools — the sheepish one and the outraged one — and the book supports both.

### How he treats them

Carelessly, and with genuine affection for what they let him do. He tests the one limit he has been told not to test, repeatedly, "for some inexplicable reason" by the builder's own account. He does not connect his behavior to the breakage. He does treat the legs as HIS — his "hoppers", his upgrades, his excitement — and he grieves the loss of each set as a personal injury rather than as broken equipment.

### What he says when they fail

**Two distinct shapes, and which one fires depends on FAULT:**

- **His fault:** exclamation → self-narrated blunder in the third person → the apology-as-inventory → formal request for assistance → then, only after all that, the unrepentant defense of hopping. Shame is claimed but never acted on.
- **Not his fault:** no apology at all. Formal protest, addressed to the whole group ("Gentlemen"), with a COUNT of how many sets he's been given, a complaint that he was led to believe they were top quality, and — at the absolute bottom — the one swear in the book.

He blames: himself (verbally, insincerely), the quality of the hardware (sincerely), and never the builders as people — he protests TO them while remaining courteous, and when Katia apologizes to him he responds by complaining that her spare parts are too small for him.

### What he says when they are new or upgraded

Quantified excitement, third person, plus physical bouncing. "Jamal is quite excited to battle with his new upgrades," Anticipation lines while WAITING for repair are a separate and useful pool: Miss Katia says she will make Jamal's new hoppers soon — mayhaps when he gets back from the fighting? **He negotiates the schedule of his own repair.**

### Quality complaints — a ready-made recurring gag

Yes, and it is the best one in the book. **"Top quality"** is his standard for legs, it is a promise he believes he was made, and it is violated every single time. The gag is load-bearing enough to carry the mod's whole leg-break flavor text, and it works better each time it repeats because the count goes up.

## How he moves and flops (mod-critical — drives PLAN C.4 walk, C.5 flop loop)

### On legs

- **Fast. Terrifyingly fast** — the narration uses that word twice, and he keeps pace with motorcycles, a chariot and mounted riders across ten miles of open ground while holding the rear of a convoy.
- **Silhouette: a "horrifying spider hybrid".** The shark body rides up on eight double-jointed legs. Both the builder and the narrator find it unsettling to look at, and the narration explicitly can't decide which version of him is scarier, spider Jamal or flopping land-shark Jamal.
- **Sound: the legs WHIR, and the feet CLICK-CLACK on hard floors** (specifically on a spiral staircase). The whir is the running sound, the click-clack is the footfall. When a set breaks: a thrashing, then a POP, then a mechanical whir, plus the smell of broken machinery and fire.
- **He climbs vertical surfaces like a spider** — straight up a wall, and holds position partway up it. He also fits on a spiral staircase that a smaller creature barely manages.
- **He was built to be QUIET as well as quick** — that was Carl's stated reason for the rig, since the legless version could be neither. Whether he actually achieves quiet is doubtful given the click-clacking.
- His movement while stationary is constant: bouncing up and down, hopping in place, hopping in circles around whatever he is guarding.

### Off legs (the flop) — and the mod has a decision to make here

**The book contradicts itself, and this matters for the beached state.** Early on, without legs, the assessment is that he is apparently pretty good at flopping but can move neither fast nor subtly — pitiful, the thing the legs were built to fix. Later, flopping legless behind a convoy of vehicles, the narration says he could actually move ALARMINGLY fast like this, and earlier still he rolls and tumbles down a hill into a battle and comes up out of the smoke with a mercenary in his mouth, looking as if he had somehow managed to jump WITHOUT legs.

Mechanically the flop is: flopping, rolling, tumbling, barrel rolls, and in combat something that reads like a lunge. Not a crawl, not a drag. He gets around and he is dangerous doing it.

**So: flopping is not slow, it is UNDIGNIFIED.** He hates it and complains constantly while doing it, and that is the punishment — not immobility. The mod should decide knowingly whether to honor the books (beached Jamal keeps most of his speed and all of his misery) or the design intent in SPEC.md (beached is a real penalty). Flagging it for B.4 rather than picking. The narratively faithful compromise: beached Jamal is slow-ish but NOISY about it, and the punishment is the apology spam.

### Jumping and hopping

- He calls it a **"little hop,"** always minimizing. It is frequent enough that the builder describes it as something he "kept doing".
- **On legs:** he hops in place constantly when excited, hops in circles, hops for no reason, and in combat performs real leaps — off a wall, landing "deftly", then a second leap with a ballerina-like twirl in midair, landing behind the party. Donut calls the twirl elegant. **So he CAN land well; the legs just can't take it twice.** The mod's landing roll is well-founded and the airborne pose should look deliberate, not flailing.
- **Height and control:** no figure is given. He is explicitly told not to jump off a tower, with the reason stated as the legs being strong but not THAT strong — a ready-made `jump refused` line.
- **Why he does it:** yearning, framed as recovering the flight he lost. He wants to feel the wind in his gills and hop across a battlefield.
- **What it costs:** everything. Three sets of legs.

### Knocked over / stuck / out of position

- Hit by a flung mob and by a child's dulled spear, he **yelps** and then flops in circles. The yelp is the damage sound.
- Being somewhere his body doesn't fit is a running problem: the trenches are not designed for him, the troop transports can't take him, the workshop parts are too small. When benched for this reason he negotiates rather than obeys.
- When he cannot move he complains LOUDLY and continuously, and keeps addressing the group as if mid-conversation.
- Heat bothers him — he removes himself to the shade and reports doing so.

### Body facts for the render

- **17 feet long**, described as overweight and oversized, otherwise the anatomy of an ordinary hammerhead shark.
- **Breathes AIR. Cannot breathe water, despite still having gills.** He is a land animal that looks like a sea animal.
- **Eight legs, double-jointed, plus a harness** that wraps the body. This matches the stock spidertron's leg count exactly — no adaptation needed.
- **Flamethrower on the UNDERBELLY**, nozzle aimable and sometimes pointed backward.
- No sense of smell (previously enhanced, now entirely gone).
- He waves his whole head back and forth to express misery — the hammerhead shape is the expressive organ. Worth a beached-state animation beat.
- Level 72 at the start of the book.

### Excitement tells (idle animation + idle lines)

Hopping in place. Bouncing up and down. Barrel rolls that send rocks flying. Spinning in circles. Quivering. **Bouncing in time to music.** And the best one for the mod: **little tendrils of flame keep leaking out of the flamethrower when he is excited.** Idle Jamal involuntarily leaks fire. That is a free, canonical, visually perfect idle effect.

## Relationships

### Who he apologizes TO, ranked

1. **Carl — "Mister Carl" / "Mr. Carl."** Builder, owner, commander. Apologized to for every broken set of legs, every blunder, every delay he causes. **This is the dynamic the mod inherits wholesale: the player is Carl.** Carl's side is affectionate exasperation — swears at him by name, calls him "buddy", pats him on the head, gives him real orders and real jobs, and keeps building him legs.
2. **Donut — "Miss Donut."** Apologized to for his verbal tics, not his damage. She scolds the third-person habit relentlessly, tries reverse psychology on him, tells him to shut up during a crisis — and also calls his midair twirl elegant, which is the warmest thing anyone says to him all book.
3. **Katia — "Miss Katia".** The good engineer. She apologizes to HIM for the legs failing, twice, and promises replacements. He responds by complaining her parts are too small. The relationship is a running inversion of who owes whom an apology.
4. **Anyone he burns.** One elaborate apology each, extended to their family, delivered in cute euphemism. They yell at him. He rearranges the nozzle.

### Others who recur

- **Mordecai:** quartermaster for legs — holds the spare set.
- **Elle:** nearly incinerated by him, calls him "shark boy", and is the only person who yells at him about the flamethrower specifically.
- **The flesh-golem children:** one smacks him with a spear by accident and apologizes to HIM, and they get assigned to help him back to base. He is roughly their social peer, which is a nice read on his standing.
- **Bigs the slug, Gonk the yak, Rend the meatball:** his cohort. The not-quite-pets, not-quite-soldiers who the system also can't classify. He gets left with them, guards things with them, bounces to music with them.
- **Nobody is cruel to him.** Exasperated, yes. Cruel, no. That is worth preserving in the mod's tone — the player should be annoyed, never encouraged to resent him.

### Standing in the group

**Deliberately ambiguous, and the book uses the ambiguity.** The system classifies him as a former-tattoo NPC — not a pet, not a crawler — which has a hard mechanical consequence: when a buff lands on everyone else, he does not get it. He IS assigned to the personal-guard platoon, IS given solo tactical assignments, IS trusted with prisoners, and IS obeyed-to when he sends messages to Carl. So: functionally a soldier, administratively a possession, socially somewhere near the children. He never comments on it. At the end he chooses to stay when he could leave, which is the book's quiet answer.

## Running gags

- **The hopping.** He hops, the legs break, he apologizes, he defends hopping, repeat. **Repeatable forever — this is the mod's core loop, already written.**
- **"Top quality".** Every new set is promised to be, none is. Repeatable, and it improves with the count.
- **Counting the sets out loud.** Repeatable and mechanically trivial: the mod already knows how many times it broke.
- **The flamethrower going off by accident.** He promises it will only ever be on purpose, then leaks flame whenever he is excited, then apologizes to somebody's family. Repeatable, and it doubles as an idle particle effect.
- **The parts are too small for Jamal.** Repeatable lament.
- **Donut policing his third person.** Needs a second speaker, so only partly portable — but *Jamal, stop that* from the player's own force console is a funny thing the mod could fake.
- **Every conversation routes back to the hopping grievance.** He derails a discussion about his lost sense of smell into the hopping. Repeatable and extremely portable to idle chatter.
- **"A most unexpected development".** He said it, then said it again later with "indeed" on the end. A callback gag he performs on himself — perfect for an anti-repeat speech system that is ALLOWED to repeat this one.

**Mod events with NO book-7 support — B.3 has to write these fresh in his voice:**

- **`died`** — he never dies and never comes close on-page. Nothing to adapt.
- **`enter` / `exit`** — nobody rides him; there is no driver-mount analogue. The closest thing is being given or denied permission to come along, which is where `jump refused` lines come from instead.
- **`moving`** — richly DESCRIBED (whirring, click-clacking, terrifying speed) but he almost never narrates his own travel. Fresh lines needed; the self-narration template makes them easy to write faithfully.
- **`low health`** on the vehicle itself — his damage complaints are all about the LEGS, never about his body. If the mod wants *I am hurt* lines they are inventions. Consider mapping vehicle damage onto leg-quality complaints instead, which is both faithful and funnier.

## Evolution within this book

He gains, in order: a third set of legs (and loses it on page one of his first scene), a stretch of legless flopping, a Katia-built fortified set, a flamethrower with a proper name, the trust to hold a solo firing position and guard prisoners, a fifth set destroyed by someone else's weapon, and a sixth and final set. **Hardware is his whole arc, and the mod is therefore already a faithful adaptation of his character arc: the legs are the story.**

What he loses: nothing new in this book — the losses (apex-predator status, flight, sense of smell) all predate it and he carries them as grievances rather than wounds.

What shows up late: standing. By the last act he is trusted with real assignments, addressed as a teammate, and when the NPCs are free to go he stays. Nothing in his voice changes to reflect it, which is the point — he was always talking like a member of the party and everyone else caught up.

**Hindsight effect:** the early scene establishes that hopping destroys the legs and that he has been told to stop. Every later hop — the excited bouncing, the midair twirl, the hops while guarding prisoners — reads as him getting away with it. He is not chastened, he is unsupervised.

## Flagged for the mod (things the template didn't ask for)

- **PLAN D.7 is CONFIRMED by the text, not just a good idea.** The flamethrower is canon: a named, donated, harness-mounted flamethrower on his underbelly, fitted by a crafting team, with an aimable nozzle. He uses it as his primary ranged weapon and clears an enclosed nest with it. **Replacing the rocket launchers is the faithful choice.** Two free bonuses the book hands the implementation: (1) the nozzle direction is a plot point, so `gun_center_shift` per direction has canon cover, and (2) it LEAKS when he is idle or excited, which justifies a small persistent flame effect on an idle jamaltron.
- **Sound design (PLAN D.6), all canonical:** a whir for the legs running, a click-clack for footfalls on hard ground, and for the break — a thrash, a POP, then a mechanical whir, with the smell of burning machinery (not reproducible, but it argues for smoke on the break FX). A yelp when damaged. A whoop plus a barrel roll on a kill. The narration never gives him a shark sound — he talks instead.
- **Eight legs, double-jointed, on a harness** — the stock spidertron leg count and topology are already correct. No prototype gymnastics needed.
- **Voice-controlled legs** is the canonical control scheme for the prototype rig, and it is a gift for the mod: the jump input can be flavored as the driver TELLING Jamal to hop, with Jamal's own line as the confirmation. The thought-reading upgrade is stated as an intention only and should not be built on.
- **He is explicitly forbidden from jumping off a tower** because the legs are strong but not that strong — canon `jump refused` grounds for refusing a jump over a cliff, a platform edge, or water.
- **He climbs vertical walls.** Out of scope for Factorio, worth knowing it's canon before anyone claims a wall-climb would be unfaithful.
- **He keeps pace with motor vehicles over ten miles.** Do not nerf his speed below the stock spidertron.
- **He is heat-averse and has no sense of smell** — free flavor for Space Age planet interactions and for a factoriopedia line.
- **He takes his own count of the legs.** If the mod tracks break count per entity, the line pool can quote the number. Highest-value cheap feature in the whole character.

## Open questions / contradictions

1. **The flop speed contradiction (needs a decision at B.4).** The text says both "couldn’t move fast" (early, legless) and "could actually move alarmingly fast like this" (late, legless). SPEC.md wants beached to be a punishment. My read: the flop is fast but humiliating and LOUD, and the punishment should be the apology spam plus loss of the weapon, not immobility. Not my call to make.
2. **The fourth set of legs.** Jamal's count says five sets by the EMP; the extract shows three Carl sets breaking and one EMP failure. Set 4's end is off-window. Doesn't change the mod, does mean *three broke from hopping* is a floor, not a total.
3. **Do the Katia legs read his thoughts?** Designed to, never confirmed. Book 8 may settle it — a merge question, not a book-7 answer.
4. **Who built the harness vs. who built the flamethrower mount?** Legs are Carl's then Katia's; the weapon mount is a third party's crafting team. If the mod credits a builder in flavor text, that seam matters.
5. **No `died` material at all.** Whatever the mod says when a jamaltron is destroyed is an invention. Given that his stated expectation is getting "smushed", a death line built on that word would be faithful in spirit and fabricated in letter — chotchki's call at B.4.
6. **Is the swear shippable?** It is one line, it is the funniest thing he says, and it is canonically reserved for maximum misery. Recommendation: gate it behind the `legs break` event at the highest verbosity tier only, and never in a common pool. Needs an explicit yes.
7. **Verbosity mapping.** The book gives register, not volume — third person for chatter, first person for sincerity. Whether the quiet/normal/unbearable tiers should prune by register or just by frequency is a design decision the text can't make.
8. **What he calls the player.** He says "Mister Carl." A mod can't know the player's name is Carl. Options: always "Mister Carl" (funny, wrong), the player's actual name with *Mister*/*Miss* prefixed (faithful to the FORM), or just "Gentlemen" for everyone (canonical, and he already uses it as a group address). **This affects every single line in the catalog, so it should be decided at B.4 before B.5 generates anything.**
