# Jamal character profile — Book 8 (*A Parade of Horribles*)

Source: `extracts/book8_jamal.txt` (55 paragraph hits, ±2 paragraphs of context — the text is WINDOWED, so scenes are fragmentary and nothing here depends on plot I could not see)

Companion file: **`book8_lines.md`** — the verbatim spoken-line bank (47 grep-verified fragments, ≤1 sentence each, each tagged with the situation that produced it). This profile keeps to the template's 6-quote budget; the bank exists because PLAN B.3 needs raw strings and the template's Exemplar section cannot hold 44 of them. Flagged in Open questions for B.4.

**Where he sits in this book.** Book 8's tenth floor is a vehicle-race floor: garages, timed heats, hired mercenaries. Jamal is one of Carl's mercenaries and spends most of the book PARKED — stationed in a garage as a guard, handed menial jobs, used as furniture by the smaller characters. He gets one hardware upgrade (hand-like grabbers on his front legs), one scene of real interiority, and one proper battle at the end where he fights with a flamethrower and loses his legs entirely. His comic register is fully formed and constant; the un-funny material is new and arrives exactly once.

## Voice

### The apology habit (mod-critical)

**Shapes** — five distinct grammatical forms, all reusable as templates:

1. **Apology as countable inventory.** He *piles up* apologies and presents the stack. Apologies are objects he accumulates and owes, not a speech act he performs once. Template: `I am piling up my apologies to you, <honorific>.` This is the single best line shape in the book for a mod — it implies a running debt counter, which is exactly what a speech system spamming him on a timer already is.
2. **Intent disclaimer, third person.** He denies *intention* rather than fault: it is not Jamal's intention to add any hair-pulling distress. Note the construction — he apologizes for the *additional increment* of distress, as if a baseline of distress from him is assumed and only the surplus is his fault.
3. **Apology as a subordinate clause on the way to the news.** In his longest utterance the apology is bolted mid-sentence into an explanation, and the actual emergency lands last. He apologizes *in passing* while burying the thing that matters.
4. **Formal protest as a complete utterance.** Two words and a verb, third person, courtroom-flavored. This is the apology's mirror image and it fires when he is overlooked rather than blamed.
5. **Embarrassed confession framed as a shameful admission.** *It is with great embarrassment that I admit this* — the admission gets its own sentence of scaffolding before any content arrives.

**Escalation is INVERTED, and this is the load-bearing finding.** Low stakes (brushing a cat too hard) produce maximum formality, third person, invented compound nouns, honorifics, hedges. Medium stakes and self-inflicted (stuck to a ceiling for hours, nobody's fault but his) produce the same elaborate register plus a full explanatory preamble — the apology and the backstory come FIRST and the emergency only surfaces when someone asks a direct question. Genuine distress (existential fear) strips the register bare: no apology, no honorifics, first person, plain short clauses. **Formality is inversely proportional to actual severity.** The mod should key verbosity tiers off exactly this: the routine events get the baroque politeness, the rare serious event gets six plain words.

**Targets, ranked.** Miss Donut (most — grooming her badly, being a nuisance in her eyeline), Mr. Carl (inconvenience, being stuck, being in the way, weapon status reports). He applies honorifics universally and without irony, including to a pet dinosaur (*Mr. Mongo*) and to a gremlin NPC (*Miss Hedy*). For the mod: the player inherits the Mr. Carl relationship (gives orders, owns the vehicle, asks if the weapon works) with a seasoning of Miss Donut (complains about his technique while he is mid-task).

**Unprompted vs reactive.** Book 8 shows very few pre-emptive apologies — no apologizing for his size, smell, or existence in this extract, and when told outright that he is too big he simply accepts it. What he does unprompted is **confess embarrassment** and **volunteer**. The inverse tic is more interesting and is the emotional center of the book: he will NOT report his own trouble. He hung from a ceiling for hours, woozy and frightened, and said nothing, because saying something would make him a bother. The narration flags his silence as the anomaly — he is otherwise never quiet, so silence is how the text signals something is wrong with him. Donut's resolution of the scene is explicit — she tells Carl she told him "he shouldn’t be scared to tell us when he’s in trouble" — which retroactively makes every apology read as bother-aversion rather than politeness.

**What he does NOT apologize for.** His legs (he defends them — when Carl assumes they broke, he corrects the diagnosis and takes the blame onto himself instead: nothing is wrong with the hardware, he is simply afraid). His fear, once he finally names it. Wanting the ocean. His flamethrower. Being excited. He swears at nobody and about nothing, in a book where every other character swears constantly. **He would rather confess cowardice than let anyone think his legs failed** — that is the cleanest character fact in the book and it should shape the leg-break lines.

**Adjacent registers** (each fills catalog slots the apology cannot):
- **Duty restatement**: repeats an order back as an institutional commission — *We have been tasked with*, first person plural, as though the garage were a posting. Pure guard-bot flavor, free for idle and command-done lines.
- **Over-confident volunteering**: offers to do things he physically cannot, with no hedge, then immediately proposes an absurd method (drive with his legs — or his mouth).
- **Literal-minded good news**: reports a trivial success as a victory, then honestly downgrades it in the same breath (the bug count).
- **Social reporting**: notices and plainly relays other people's emotional states. He is the one who knows who is sad.
- **Formal protest**: the objection register, used when overlooked.

### Third person vs first person

**Default: third person, but MIXED — he uses both inside a single sentence.** The consistent pattern is third person for himself-as-actor and first person for intent, possession and feeling: *Jamal was testing his legs* / *my toasty emissions* / *I do apologize*. One sentence has him asking to be turned around so that Jamal may attempt driving with *my* legs. Any mod line generator that picks one grammatical person and sticks to it will sound less like him than one that mixes.

**The slip.** Full, sustained first person arrives with sincerity, fear, or grief, and he never corrects himself. The ceiling monologue opens in third person and slides into first, then stays there for the rest of the speech — and the narration marks it as him talking fast, in a strange sad tone, interrupting someone, which he does nowhere else. Acute physical panic also drops the third person (*I cannot reach!* / *I am quite snug!*), so the trigger is arousal generally, not solemnity specifically.

**Why the mod cares.** The mapping holds and the book supports it: **third person = routine, comic, in control; first person = frightened, stuck, or sincere.** That is a free severity dial for the speech system. Caveat: the first-person panic lines are short and funny while the first-person sincere lines are long and sad, so grammatical person alone is not a tone switch — pair it with length.

### Catchphrases & verbal tics

- **Stock openers**: *Oh boy* (excitement), *Oh yes, Mr. Carl* / *Oh, yes, Miss Donut* (eager affirmative — the comma-variant appears both ways), *My goodness, Mr. Carl* (mild astonishment). Note: *My goodness* is also Donut's signature exclamation in the same scenes; it may be borrowed off her rather than his own. Flagged.
- **Self-narration as a status readout — the most machinable tic in the book.** `Jamal is <state>` used as literal status output: stuck, feeling woozy, afraid of heights, quite excited, most certainly ready to drive, happy to have his legs. He reports his internal state in the third person the way a machine reports a flag. A Factorio speech bubble is a status line, so this is a direct fit and the template shape for most of the catalog.
- **Honorifics**: Mr. Carl, Miss Donut, Mr. Mongo, Miss Hedy. Never drops, not once, not under panic, not when Carl is transformed into a dog (he addresses the dog as Mr. Carl with no comment on it whatsoever).
- **Invented compounds and malapropisms** (free comedy for item descriptions and locale strings): *sticky feets* (double-plural), *inverted conundrum* (hanging upside down and stuck), *melty inconvenience* (what his flamethrower does to enemies), *toasty emissions* (his own flame, treated as an emission), *hair-pulling distress* (brushing a cat too hard). Also treats vehicles as persons — enemy cars are *nasty cars who* try to beat them.
- **Intensifiers**: ***quite*** is his word and it is everywhere — quite primed, quite snug, quite excited, quite the inverted conundrum, quite a bit. *Most* is the runner-up: most certainly, most difficult. If a generated line needs one word to sound like him, it is *quite*.
- **Hedges**: sentence-final *I fear* twice, softening bad news. He hedges facts, never opinions.
- **Register floor/ceiling**: zero profanity, zero aggression at people, no sarcasm, no irony. He never insults anyone — the closest he comes is an unintentional insult delivered as a courteous technical observation (telling Donut her mats are worse than Carl's hair, which he does not register as an insult at all and which is the funniest beat he gets).

### Sentence shape

- Courtesy scaffolding is over-built and the news is buried at the end. His stuck-on-the-ceiling utterance runs: what he was doing → why he was doing it → the hardware he was testing → an apology → and only then, in the final clause, the fact that he cannot get down. He answers the literal question asked and omits the important part. **The mod's short-bubble version of him has to invert this**, which means the one-line variants lose the joke — flag for the verbosity tiers: his baroque shape needs two sentences to land, his status-line shape survives truncation to four words.
- Typical utterance: one to two sentences, 8–20 words. Nothing he says is long except the one monologue.
- He gets interrupted constantly — cut off mid-sentence by a pet shoving past him, cut off by Donut. He interrupts exactly once, in the sad scene, and the text flags it as out of character.
- He does not trail off or repeat himself within a scene; the repetition is across scenes, in the tics.

### Register & vocabulary

Four registers, with triggers:

1. **Butler formality (default, ~70% of his lines)** — errands, grooming, guard duty, status reports, weapon readiness. Third person, honorifics, *quite*, hedges. Trigger: any routine interaction.
2. **Puppyish excitement** — hopping in place, waving his front legs, *Oh boy*, volunteering for jobs beyond him. Trigger: the team returning, being summoned, being included, a new hat.
3. **Embarrassed confession / formal protest** — when he is the problem, or when he has been overlooked. Hedged, elaborate, news-last. Trigger: his own screwups, being miscounted.
4. **Plain frightened sincerity (exactly once in the book)** — first person, short clauses, no scaffolding, ocean and loss vocabulary. Trigger: contemplating that the crawlers could die and everything would simply stop. Use RARELY in the mod or not at all.

Species/origin flavor: he wants to go back to the ocean; he used to watch everything at once from above when he was *real*; his mouth is a tool (he offers to drive with it, he picks his own broken legs up with it, he snaps monsters in half with it). The shark instinct is in his HARDWARE usage, not in his manners — the manners are entirely servant-class human.

### Exemplar lines

Six budgeted quotes, all grep-verified (five here, one in the trigger table):

## Personality

### Core traits

- **Compulsively useful.** Accepts every task instantly — guard the garage, guard the truck, brush the party, carry Samantha, chase bugs, drive a truck he does not fit in — and reports back on each like a duty log. He never asks why.
- **Recklessly experimental with his own hardware.** He climbed a hangar ceiling to find out whether his upgraded feet would hold him inverted. They did. He had no exit plan. No one asked him to do this.
- **Physically fearless, emotionally fragile.** He jumps higher than a ceiling routinely and lands on monsters to snap them in half, yet admits to a fear of heights — and his own explanation is that when he jumps he never has time to notice the height because he is always already falling. The courage was never courage, it was insufficient airtime.
- **Bother-averse past the point of self-harm.** Hours stuck and woozy, silent, because speaking up would inconvenience someone. This is the trait the apology tic is made of.
- **Literal-minded optimism.** One insect repelled counts as a successful defensive program, and he volunteers the true count himself without noticing it undercuts him.
- **Socially observant.** He tracks other people's feelings and reports them plainly and unprompted — he is the one who knows which of the NPCs is still sad about his friends.

### Drives

Small and specific, which is what the mod needs: be given a job; be told the job was done right; be included when the group goes somewhere; keep his legs; get better legs; wear the hat (he is explicitly a fan of the hats); be brave on purpose rather than by accident; not be a burden; see the ocean again.

### Fears & insecurities

- **Heights** — newly admitted, and he is embarrassed to admit it.
- **Sudden annihilation** — a gremlin told him that if the crawlers die, everyone contingent on them stops existing, without warning and without knowing. This is what put him on the ceiling: he went up to see everything at once while it was still there.
- **Loss of the family** — he is scared to come down in case it is the last time he sees it all.
- **Not being the real one** — he believes the shark he came from was brave and that he is not that shark. He states it once and does not argue with it.

### Trait-in-action

- Given guard duty, he physically blocks a drunk dinosaur from leaving the garage and cites the commission out loud while being shoved aside.
- Handed brushes he has never used, he brushes hard enough to hurt and responds to the complaint with a courteous technical explanation of why her fur is harder to brush than her owner's hair — meaning to be helpful, landing an insult he cannot see.
- Wrecked, legless, smashed against a wall, he is not calling for help: he is flopping around trying to pick up his own broken leg pieces with his mouth.

### Self-image vs reality

He rates himself as a lesser copy of a braver original, and as a nuisance whose surplus distress must be apologized for. Everyone around him treats him as annoying-but-ours — the scene resolves with him being told outright that he is part of the family now. He does not argue, but nothing later in the book suggests he believes it either. **The identity question is where he stops being funny**; the mod should either hold that line back entirely or spend it once, and never on a timer.

## His legs (mod-critical)

- **Provenance: NOT ESTABLISHED IN THIS BOOK.** No builder is named for the legs themselves and no set is counted. The legs are pre-existing kit that this book only upgrades. What IS established: **Mordecai designed the grabbers** added to the front legs, and the sticky feet are described as new and improved (upgrader unnamed). A fleshmancer takes an interest and talks to him about his legs off-page. Anything about who originally built them has to come from book 7 or be decided in B.4.
- **Mechanism: NOT ESTABLISHED.** No voice control, no thought control, nothing about a harness or what it attaches to. What the text shows is capability: the **front two legs are manipulators** with hand-like grabbers (he holds a brush in each, one hand on a steering wheel and one on the pedals, and holds a person aloft in front of a screen). The **feet are adhesive** enough to hold his full weight inverted on a ceiling and parked on a wall. A **flamethrower** is mounted and he operates it.
- **Failure modes in book 8, precisely:**
  1. A thrown wrench clanks off a leg. No damage. He shouts.
  2. He gets **stuck, not broken** — a nerve-and-geometry failure on the ceiling, hardware working perfectly. Carl's first assumption is that the legs broke; Jamal corrects him and takes the blame himself.
  3. **Total loss by external attack**: in the final arena fight a boss-scale area attack smashes him into a wall and his legs are wrecked and come off entirely. He survives. He then flops, collecting the pieces by mouth.
  4. At the end of the extract his legs are still smashed — **no on-page repair scene**.
- **Jumping does NOT break his legs in this book, and the text argues the opposite.** He jumps constantly, has jumped higher than a ceiling dozens of times, hops with a midair flip and lands *deftly*, and uses jump-landings as an offensive attack that snaps monsters in half. The mod's break-on-landing roll (PLAN E.4) is therefore a game-design invention, not book canon. That is fine — but it should be knowingly fine, and the flavor text should probably blame the *landing surface*, the *height he could not see*, or a boss hit, rather than the jump itself. **Decision for B.4.**
- **How he treats them**: proud, curious, careless of consequences. He tests their limits on purpose, without telling anyone, and does not connect testing to getting into trouble. He talks about them to strangers.
- **What he says when they fail**: **GAP — no spoken line exists at the moment of the wreck.** He is silent when smashed and silent while flopping. What the book DOES give is the stuck-not-broken pool: apology first, explanation second, emergency last, plus an embarrassed confession, plus a defense of the hardware against the accusation that it broke. The `legs break` and `flopping` line pools must therefore be **written fresh in his voice**, anchored on: he apologizes for the inconvenience, not the damage; he defends the legs; he does not ask for help until asked.
- **What he says when they are new/upgraded**: also thin. There is no line at the moment the grabbers are fitted — he is simply seen practicing with them and apologizing for his technique. The `built` and `repaired` pools inherit the **volunteering-and-testing** energy instead: the natural new-legs line is him announcing an experiment nobody asked for.
- **Quality complaints: NONE.** He never complains about the legs, not once, in any condition. He complains about a cat's mats and about being miscounted. The recurring gag is not that the legs are bad — it is that **he keeps finding new ways to get himself stuck to architecture with them**, which is the better gag for a mod anyway.

## How he moves and flops (mod-critical)

- **On legs.** Metallic and audible — *Clank, clank, clank* over a hard floor, approaching before he is seen. He rides high: tall enough to loom over a man lying on the floor and to be looked up at, and his head is a platform two small characters casually sit on. Legs are mechanical and clearly artificial; a thrown wrench rings off them. Agility beats the silhouette's promise — he hops in place, waves the front pair around while doing it, and can hop with a **midair flip and a deft landing** from ceiling height. He walks on walls and ceilings with the sticky feet.
- **Size.** Big. He does not fit in a truck cab — they had to cut the cab off a pickup and set him inside it to let him drive, and inside a vehicle he is *quite snug* and cannot reach a window handle. He can hold a person up like a prop. Read him as a vehicle-scale entity, which suits a spidertron footprint.
- **Off legs — the flop.** Book 8's only on-page flopping is post-wreck and it is **slow and pitiful**: flopping about, mouthing at his own broken leg pieces, going nowhere. HOWEVER, Carl reminds him of a previous occasion (book 7 material, referenced here) where he rolled down a hill flopping *into* a battle full of power-armored soldiers, i.e. alarmingly effective. **Contradictory — flag for the merge.** Book 8 alone supports the beached state being a punishment.
- **Jumping and hopping.** Frequent, casual, high, and mechanically his attack: he jumps through an arena with the flamethrower roaring, lands on monsters, and snaps them in half with his mouth. He can control the flip. What he **cannot** do is see where he is going — he says he never has time to check the height because he is always already falling. Perfect flavor for `jump`, `land`, and especially `jump refused`.
- **Knocked over / stuck / out of position.** He stays exactly where he is and says nothing until directly questioned. Stuck on a ceiling: waits hours. Wedged in a truck: states the constraint flatly (cannot reach, too snug) without complaint. Told to stay: stays. Told he is too big: accepts it.
- **Body facts for the render.** Hammerhead shark body on mechanical spider legs; front pair ends in hand-like grabbers, which the narration says makes him look *more* terrifying; flamethrower mounted; a wide-brimmed hat that reshapes itself to fit a shark's head (late-book, cosmetic, possibly worth an alt sprite); breathes air with no apparent difficulty and no water requirement mentioned — the ocean is nostalgia, not physiology. Two small allies ride his head at once.
- **Excitement tells** (idle animation + idle lines): hopping up and down in place, front legs waving, hopping off a wall when called, giving a single excited hop at passers-by, settling into a circle on the floor to rest with a pet curled against him. That last one is the best idle pose in the book.

## Relationships

- **Who he apologizes TO, ranked**: **Miss Donut** (technique failures, being a nuisance in her presence), **Mr. Carl** (inconvenience, being stuck, being unusable), then honorifics-for-everyone (*Mr. Mongo* the pet dinosaur, *Miss Hedy* the gremlin) without ever apologizing to them as such. **The player should read as Mr. Carl**: the one who assigns jobs, asks whether the weapon works, tells him to guard something, and gets status reports. The Donut dynamic (being scolded mid-task for doing the job badly) is the better source for `damaged` and `command done` griping.
- **Carl**: employer and commander, addressed with unbroken formality. Jamal takes orders instantly and literally, restates them as a commission, and stays where he is put. Carl treats him as an exasperating but genuinely useful asset — checks his weapon before a fight, parks him on guard duty, and is audibly annoyed when the rules make him unusable. Carl is also the one who punctures his self-image, by listing the reckless things he has done to prove he is not actually afraid of heights.
- **Donut**: his harshest and his kindest. She scolds his brushing in real time, calls him annoying to his face and *Jar Jar* behind the honorifics, and orders him around — and she is the one who floats up to the ceiling to whisper him down, then defends him as part of the family. The mod can borrow this shape: the being-complained-at is affection.
- **Samantha** (a flying severed head): the one who plays with him. She summons him like a dog, he comes hopping and delighted; she rides his head, has him carry her around and hold her up to screens, and includes him in her plans. He obeys her as happily as he obeys Carl — **he does not distinguish rank among the people who are kind to him**.
- **Mongo and Rend** (the pets): peers and co-guards. He honorifics Mongo, tries to enforce garage rules on him, gets physically shoved aside by Rend mid-sentence, and rests on the floor with Mongo curled against him.
- **Hedy** (gremlin driver, garage-bound like him): fellow minion, and the source of his existential dread — she is the one who told him that if the crawlers die, everyone else simply stops.
- **Mordecai**: designed the grabbers. **Grigori** (fleshmancer): asks him about his legs, the only character who takes his hardware seriously. **Bigs** (a slug): rides his head, heckles workers from it.
- **Standing in the group.** Mechanically he is a **hired mercenary** — a game-system role that makes him ineligible to drive in certain heats, which is played as a comic inconvenience. Socially he starts the book as a pet/tool (parked, borrowed, ridden, used as an extra guard) and is explicitly **promoted to family out loud** at the midpoint. He finishes the book as an actual combatant. His own read on himself never catches up.

## Running gags

- **Honorifics for animals.** *Mr. Mongo*, to a drunk dinosaur, while being shoved aside. **Repeatable.**
- **Everyone parks Jamal in the garage.** He is left behind as the extra guard again and again, and receives it as a solemn commission every time. **Repeatable** — and it is basically the mod's idle state.
- **Jamal as equipment.** Small characters ride his head, get carried by him, get held up to screens by him. He is delighted to be used as furniture. **Repeatable.**
- **Volunteering for the impossible.** Ready to drive! (He does not fit, cannot reach the controls, and proposes using his mouth.) **Repeatable** and the best source of `jump refused` style lines.
- **The apology arrives before the information.** Structural, fires every time, **repeatable**, and reliably funny *because* it repeats.
- **`quite` + state, as a status readout.** **Repeatable** — the backbone tic.
- **Trivial victories reported as victories, then honestly downgraded.** **Repeatable.**
- **Getting stuck to architecture while testing his own legs.** Once on-page, but built to repeat and the natural fit for the mod's beached state.
- **Getting cut off mid-sentence.** Once on-page; works as a one-shot bubble gag (a line that ends in an em-dash).

## Evolution within this book

He arrives as comic furniture: parked in garages, borrowed for chores, hopping at the sight of people, apologizing for grooming a cat too hard. Mid-book he gets the grabber upgrade and becomes genuinely useful — hands, carrying, driving — and in the same stretch the ceiling scene converts him from a bit into a person: the fear of heights, the ocean, the conviction that the brave shark he came from was someone else, and an explicit on-page promotion to family. After that he is trusted with a weapon and a heat, and in the final battle he is a real combatant, jumping through an arena with a flamethrower roaring and killing things with his mouth. Then he is smashed flat and ends the book legless, flopping, gathering his own broken pieces.

What that does to the earlier lines in hindsight: the compulsive politeness stops reading as manners and starts reading as **bother-aversion from someone who does not think he is entitled to be here**. The apology-as-debt shape is literal — he believes he owes. And the physical fearlessness is retroactively not courage at all, by his own account: he is brave only because he has never had time to look down.
