# Jamal character profile — Book 8 (*A Parade of Horribles*)

Source: `extracts/book8_jamal.txt` (55 paragraph hits, ±2 paragraphs of context — the text is WINDOWED, so scenes are fragmentary and nothing here depends on plot I could not see)

Companion file: **`book8_lines.md`** — the verbatim spoken-line bank (47 grep-verified fragments, ≤1 sentence each, each tagged with the situation that produced it). This profile keeps to the template's 6-quote budget; the bank exists because PLAN B.3 needs raw strings and the Exemplar section cannot hold 44 of them. Flagged in Open questions for B.4.

**Where he sits in this book.** Floor ten is a vehicle-race floor: garages, timed heats, hired mercenaries. Jamal is one of Carl's mercenaries and spends most of the book PARKED — a garage guard, handed menial jobs, used as furniture by the smaller characters. He gets one hardware upgrade (hand-like grabbers on his front legs), one scene of real interiority and one proper battle at the end where he fights with a flamethrower and loses his legs entirely. The comic register is fully formed and constant; the un-funny material is new and arrives exactly once.

## Voice

### The apology habit (mod-critical)

**Shapes** — five distinct grammatical forms, all reusable as templates:

1. **Apology as countable inventory.** He *piles up* apologies and presents the stack: objects he accumulates and owes, not a speech act performed once. Template: `I am piling up my apologies to you, <honorific>.` The best line shape in the book for the mod, because it implies a running debt counter, which is exactly what a speech system spamming him on a timer already is.
2. **Intent disclaimer, third person.** He denies *intention* rather than fault: it is not Jamal's intention to add any hair-pulling distress. He apologizes for the *additional increment* of distress, as if a baseline from him is assumed and only the surplus is his fault.
3. **Apology as a subordinate clause on the way to the news.** In his longest utterance the apology is bolted mid-sentence into an explanation and the emergency lands last. He apologizes *in passing* while burying the thing that matters.
4. **Formal protest as a complete utterance.** Two words and a verb, third person, courtroom-flavored. The apology's mirror image; it fires when he is overlooked rather than blamed.
5. **Embarrassed confession framed as a shameful admission.** *It is with great embarrassment that I admit this* — the admission gets its own sentence of scaffolding before any content arrives.

**Escalation is INVERTED.** Low stakes (brushing a cat too hard) get maximum formality: third person, invented compound nouns, honorifics, hedges. Medium, self-inflicted stakes (stuck to a ceiling for hours, nobody's fault but his) get the same register plus a full explanatory preamble — apology and backstory FIRST, the emergency only when someone asks a direct question. Genuine distress (existential fear) strips it bare: no apology, no honorifics, first person, plain short clauses. Formality is inversely proportional to actual severity, so the verbosity tiers should key off exactly this: baroque politeness on the routine events, six plain words on the rare serious one.

**Targets, ranked.** Miss Donut (most — grooming her badly, being a nuisance in her eyeline), then Mr. Carl (inconvenience, being stuck, being in the way, weapon status reports). Honorifics go on everyone without irony, including a pet dinosaur (*Mr. Mongo*) and a gremlin NPC (*Miss Hedy*). For the mod the player inherits the Mr. Carl relationship (gives orders, owns the vehicle, asks if the weapon works) with a seasoning of Miss Donut (complains about his technique mid-task).

**Unprompted vs reactive.** Very few pre-emptive apologies — none for his size, smell or existence in this extract, and told outright he is too big, he simply accepts it. What he does unprompted is confess embarrassment and volunteer. The inverse tic is the emotional center of the book: he will NOT report his own trouble. He hung from a ceiling for hours, woozy and frightened, and said nothing, because saying something would make him a bother. The narration flags his silence as the anomaly (he is otherwise never quiet, so silence is how the text signals something is wrong). Donut resolves the scene explicitly — she tells Carl she told him "he shouldn’t be scared to tell us when he’s in trouble" — which retroactively makes every apology read as bother-aversion rather than politeness.

**What he does NOT apologize for.** His legs (he defends them — when Carl assumes they broke, he corrects the diagnosis and takes the blame himself: nothing is wrong with the hardware, he is simply afraid). His fear, once he finally names it. Wanting the ocean. His flamethrower. Being excited. He swears at nobody and about nothing, in a book where every other character swears constantly. He would rather confess cowardice than let anyone think his legs failed — the cleanest character fact in the book, and it should shape the leg-break lines.

**Adjacent registers** (each fills catalog slots the apology cannot):
- **Duty restatement**: an order repeated back as an institutional commission — *We have been tasked with*, first person plural, as though the garage were a posting. Pure guard-bot flavor, free for idle and command-done lines.
- **Over-confident volunteering**: offers to do things he physically cannot, no hedge, then immediately proposes an absurd method (drive with his legs, or his mouth).
- **Literal-minded good news**: reports a trivial success as a victory, then honestly downgrades it in the same breath (the bug count).
- **Social reporting**: notices and plainly relays other people's emotional states. He is the one who knows who is sad.
- **Formal protest**: the objection register, used when overlooked.

### Third person vs first person

**Default: third person, but MIXED inside a single sentence.** Third person for himself-as-actor, first person for intent, possession and feeling: *Jamal was testing his legs* / *my toasty emissions* / *I do apologize*. One sentence has him asking to be turned around so that Jamal may attempt driving with *my* legs. A line generator that picks one grammatical person and sticks to it will sound less like him than one that mixes.

**The slip.** Full, sustained first person arrives with sincerity, fear or grief, and he never corrects himself. The ceiling monologue opens in third person, slides into first and stays there — and the narration marks him talking fast, in a strange sad tone, interrupting someone, which he does nowhere else. Acute physical panic also drops the third person (*I cannot reach!* / *I am quite snug!*), so the trigger is arousal generally, not solemnity specifically.

**Why the mod cares.** The book supports the mapping: third person = routine, comic, in control; first person = frightened, stuck or sincere. That is a free severity dial. Caveat: the first-person PANIC lines are short and funny while the first-person SINCERE lines are long and sad, so grammatical person alone is not a tone switch — pair it with length.

### Catchphrases & verbal tics

- **Stock openers**: *Oh boy* (excitement), *Oh yes, Mr. Carl* / *Oh, yes, Miss Donut* (eager affirmative, spelled with and without the comma), *My goodness, Mr. Carl* (mild astonishment). *My goodness* is also Donut's signature exclamation in the same scenes, so it may be borrowed off her. Flagged.
- **Self-narration as a status readout — the most machinable tic in the book.** `Jamal is <state>` as literal status output: stuck, feeling woozy, afraid of heights, quite excited, most certainly ready to drive, happy to have his legs. He reports internal state the way a machine reports a flag. A Factorio speech bubble IS a status line, so this is a direct fit and the template shape for most of the catalog.
- **Honorifics**: Mr. Carl, Miss Donut, Mr. Mongo, Miss Hedy. Never drops — not once, not under panic, not when Carl is transformed into a dog (he addresses the dog as Mr. Carl with no comment whatsoever).
- **Invented compounds and malapropisms** (free comedy for item descriptions and locale strings): *sticky feets* (double plural), *inverted conundrum* (hanging upside down and stuck), *melty inconvenience* (what his flamethrower does to enemies), *toasty emissions* (his own flame, as an emission), *hair-pulling distress* (brushing a cat too hard). He also treats vehicles as persons — enemy cars are *nasty cars who* try to beat them.
- **Intensifiers**: *quite* is his word and it is everywhere — quite primed, quite snug, quite excited, quite the inverted conundrum, quite a bit. *Most* is the runner-up (most certainly, most difficult). If a generated line needs one word to sound like him, it is *quite*.
- **Hedges**: sentence-final *I fear*, twice, softening bad news. He hedges facts, never opinions.
- **Register floor/ceiling**: zero profanity, zero aggression at people, no sarcasm, no irony. He never insults anyone; the closest is an unintentional insult delivered as a courteous technical observation (Donut's mats are worse than Carl's hair), which he does not register as an insult and which is the funniest beat he gets.

### Sentence shape

- Courtesy scaffolding is over-built and the news is buried at the END. The stuck-on-the-ceiling utterance runs: what he was doing → why → the hardware he was testing → an apology → and only in the final clause, the fact that he cannot get down. He answers the literal question and omits the important part. A short-bubble version of him has to invert this, so the one-line variants lose the joke — flag for the verbosity tiers: the baroque shape needs two sentences to land, the status-line shape survives truncation to four words.
- Typical utterance: one to two sentences, 8–20 words. Nothing is long except the one monologue.
- He gets interrupted constantly (cut off mid-sentence by a pet shoving past, cut off by Donut). He interrupts exactly once, in the sad scene, and the text flags it as out of character.
- He does not trail off or repeat himself within a scene; the repetition is across scenes, in the tics.

### Register & vocabulary

Four registers, with triggers:

1. **Butler formality (default, ~70% of his lines)** — errands, grooming, guard duty, status reports, weapon readiness. Third person, honorifics, *quite*, hedges. Trigger: any routine interaction.
2. **Puppyish excitement** — hopping in place, waving his front legs, *Oh boy*, volunteering for jobs beyond him. Trigger: the team returning, being summoned, being included, a new hat.
3. **Embarrassed confession / formal protest** — when he is the problem, or has been overlooked. Hedged, elaborate, news-last. Trigger: his own screwups, being miscounted.
4. **Plain frightened sincerity (exactly once in the book)** — first person, short clauses, no scaffolding, ocean and loss vocabulary. Trigger: contemplating that the crawlers could die and everything would simply stop. Use RARELY in the mod or not at all.

Species/origin flavor: he wants to go back to the ocean; he used to watch everything at once from above when he was *real*; his mouth is a tool (he offers to drive with it, picks up his own broken legs with it, snaps monsters in half with it). The shark instinct lives in his HARDWARE usage, not his manners — the manners are entirely servant-class human.

## Personality

### Core traits

- **Compulsively useful.** Accepts every task instantly — guard the garage, guard the truck, brush the party, carry Samantha, chase bugs, drive a truck he does not fit in — and reports back on each like a duty log. Never asks why.
- **Recklessly experimental with his own hardware.** Climbed a hangar ceiling to find out whether his upgraded feet would hold him inverted. They did. No exit plan, and nobody asked him to.
- **Physically fearless, emotionally fragile.** Jumps higher than a ceiling routinely and lands on monsters to snap them in half, yet admits to a fear of heights — and his own explanation is that he never has time to notice the height because he is always already falling. The courage was never courage, it was insufficient airtime.
- **Bother-averse past the point of self-harm.** Hours stuck and woozy, silent, because speaking up would inconvenience someone. This is what the apology tic is made of.
- **Literal-minded optimism.** One insect repelled counts as a successful defensive program, and he volunteers the true count himself without noticing it undercuts him.
- **Socially observant.** Tracks other people's feelings and reports them plainly and unprompted — he knows which NPC is still sad about his friends.

### Drives

Small and specific, which is what the mod needs: be given a job; be told it was done right; be included when the group goes somewhere; keep his legs; get better legs; wear the hat (he is explicitly a fan of the hats); be brave on purpose rather than by accident; not be a burden; see the ocean again.

### Fears & insecurities

- **Heights** — newly admitted, and embarrassed to admit it.
- **Sudden annihilation** — a gremlin told him that if the crawlers die, everyone contingent on them stops existing, without warning and without knowing. That is what put him on the ceiling: he went up to see everything at once while it was still there.
- **Loss of the family** — he is scared to come down in case it is the last time he sees it all.
- **Not being the real one** — he believes the shark he came from was brave and that he is not that shark. He states it once and does not argue with it.

### Trait-in-action

- On guard duty, he physically blocks a drunk dinosaur from leaving the garage and cites the commission out loud while being shoved aside.
- Handed brushes he has never used, he brushes hard enough to hurt and answers the complaint with a courteous technical explanation of why her fur is harder to brush than her owner's hair — meaning to help, landing an insult he cannot see.
- Wrecked, legless, smashed against a wall, he does not call for help: he flops around trying to pick up his own broken leg pieces with his mouth.

### Self-image vs reality

He rates himself as a lesser copy of a braver original, and as a nuisance whose surplus distress must be apologized for. Everyone around him treats him as annoying-but-ours, and the scene resolves with him told outright that he is family now. He does not argue, and nothing later suggests he believes it either. The identity question is where he stops being funny: hold that line back entirely or spend it once, never on a timer.

## His legs (mod-critical)

- **Provenance: NOT ESTABLISHED IN THIS BOOK.** No builder named for the legs themselves, no set counted; they are pre-existing kit this book only upgrades. What IS established: Mordecai designed the grabbers on the front legs, and the sticky feet are new and improved (upgrader unnamed). A fleshmancer asks about his legs off-page. Original builder has to come from book 7 or be decided at B.4.
- **Mechanism: NOT ESTABLISHED.** No voice control, no thought control, nothing about a harness. The text shows capability only: the front two legs are manipulators with hand-like grabbers (a brush in each, one hand on a steering wheel and one on the pedals, a person held aloft in front of a screen); the feet are adhesive enough to hold his full weight inverted on a ceiling and to park him on a wall; a flamethrower is mounted and he operates it.
- **Failure modes in book 8, precisely:**
  1. A thrown wrench clanks off a leg. No damage. He shouts.
  2. He gets STUCK, not broken — a nerve-and-geometry failure on the ceiling, hardware working perfectly. Carl assumes the legs broke; Jamal corrects him and takes the blame himself.
  3. **Total loss by external attack**: in the final arena fight a boss-scale area attack smashes him into a wall and his legs come off entirely. He survives, then flops, collecting the pieces by mouth.
  4. At the end of the extract his legs are still smashed — no on-page repair scene.
- **Jumping does NOT break his legs in this book; the text argues the opposite.** He jumps constantly, has jumped higher than a ceiling dozens of times, hops with a midair flip and lands *deftly*, and uses jump-landings as an attack that snaps monsters in half. The mod's break-on-landing roll (PLAN E.4) is a game-design invention, not book canon. Fine if it is a knowing choice — the flavor text should probably blame the *landing surface*, the *height he could not see* or a boss hit rather than the jump itself. Decision for B.4.
- **How he treats them**: proud, curious, careless of consequences. Tests their limits on purpose without telling anyone, does not connect testing to getting into trouble, talks about them to strangers.
- **What he says when they fail**: GAP — no spoken line at the moment of the wreck. He is silent when smashed and silent while flopping. The book DOES give the stuck-not-broken pool: apology first, explanation second, emergency last, an embarrassed confession and a defense of the hardware against the accusation that it broke. So `legs break` and `flopping` must be written fresh in his voice, anchored on: he apologizes for the inconvenience, not the damage; he defends the legs; he does not ask for help until asked.
- **What he says when they are new/upgraded**: also thin. No line when the grabbers are fitted — he is only seen practicing with them and apologizing for his technique. `built` and `repaired` inherit the volunteering-and-testing energy instead: the natural new-legs line is him announcing an experiment nobody asked for.
- **Quality complaints: NONE.** Not once, in any condition. He complains about a cat's mats and about being miscounted. The recurring gag is not that the legs are bad, it is that he keeps finding new ways to get himself stuck to architecture with them, which is the better gag for a mod anyway.

## How he moves and flops (mod-critical)

- **On legs.** Metallic and audible — *Clank, clank, clank* over a hard floor, heard before he is seen. He rides high: tall enough to loom over a man on the floor, and his head is a platform two small characters casually sit on. The legs are clearly artificial; a thrown wrench rings off them. More agile than the silhouette promises — he hops in place, waves the front pair while doing it and can hop with a midair flip and a deft landing from ceiling height. Walks on walls and ceilings with the sticky feet.
- **Size.** Big. He does not fit in a truck cab (they cut the cab off a pickup and set him inside it to drive), and inside a vehicle he is *quite snug* and cannot reach a window handle. He can hold a person up like a prop. Read him as a vehicle-scale entity, which suits a spidertron footprint.
- **Off legs — the flop.** Book 8's only on-page flopping is post-wreck and SLOW and pitiful: flopping about, mouthing at his broken leg pieces, going nowhere. BUT Carl recalls an earlier occasion (book 7 material) where he rolled down a hill flopping *into* a battle full of power-armored soldiers — alarmingly effective. **Contradictory; flag for the merge.** Book 8 alone supports the beached state being a punishment.
- **Jumping and hopping.** Frequent, casual, high and mechanically his attack: he jumps through an arena with the flamethrower roaring, lands on monsters and snaps them in half with his mouth. He can control the flip. What he CANNOT do is see where he is going — he says he never has time to check the height because he is always already falling. Perfect flavor for `jump`, `land` and especially `jump refused`.
- **Knocked over / stuck / out of position.** He stays exactly where he is and says nothing until directly questioned. Stuck on a ceiling: waits hours. Wedged in a truck: states the constraint flatly (cannot reach, too snug) without complaint. Told to stay: stays. Told he is too big: accepts it.
- **Body facts for the render.** Hammerhead shark body on mechanical spider legs; the front pair ends in hand-like grabbers, which the narration says makes him look *more* terrifying; flamethrower mounted; a wide-brimmed hat that reshapes itself to fit a shark's head (late-book, cosmetic, possibly an alt sprite); breathes air with no apparent difficulty and no water requirement mentioned — the ocean is nostalgia, not physiology. Two small allies ride his head at once.
- **Excitement tells** (idle animation + idle lines): hopping up and down in place, front legs waving, hopping off a wall when called, a single excited hop at passers-by and settling into a circle on the floor with a pet curled against him — the best idle pose in the book.

## Relationships

- **Who he apologizes TO, ranked**: **Miss Donut** (technique failures, being a nuisance near her), **Mr. Carl** (inconvenience, being stuck, being unusable), then honorifics for everyone (*Mr. Mongo* the pet dinosaur, *Miss Hedy* the gremlin) without ever apologizing to them as such. The player should read as Mr. Carl: assigns jobs, asks whether the weapon works, tells him to guard something, gets the status reports. The Donut dynamic (scolded mid-task for doing the job badly) is the better source for `damaged` and `command done` griping.
- **Carl**: employer and commander, addressed with unbroken formality. Jamal takes orders instantly and literally, restates them as a commission and stays where he is put. Carl treats him as an exasperating but genuinely useful asset — checks his weapon before a fight, parks him on guard duty, is audibly annoyed when the rules make him unusable. Carl also punctures his self-image, listing the reckless things he has done to prove he is not actually afraid of heights.
- **Donut**: his harshest and his kindest. She scolds his brushing in real time, calls him annoying to his face and *Jar Jar* behind the honorifics, orders him around — and she floats up to the ceiling to whisper him down, then defends him as family. The mod can borrow the shape: being complained at IS the affection.
- **Samantha** (a flying severed head): the one who plays with him. She summons him like a dog and he comes hopping and delighted; she rides his head, has him carry her and hold her up to screens, includes him in her plans. He obeys her as happily as he obeys Carl — he does not distinguish rank among the people who are kind to him.
- **Mongo and Rend** (the pets): peers and co-guards. He honorifics Mongo, tries to enforce garage rules on him, gets shoved aside by Rend mid-sentence and rests on the floor with Mongo curled against him.
- **Hedy** (gremlin driver, garage-bound like him): fellow minion, and the source of his existential dread — she told him that if the crawlers die, everyone else simply stops.
- **Mordecai**: designed the grabbers. **Grigori** (fleshmancer): asks about his legs, the only character who takes his hardware seriously. **Bigs** (a slug): rides his head and heckles workers from it.
- **Standing in the group.** Mechanically a **hired mercenary** — a game-system role that makes him ineligible to drive in certain heats, played as a comic inconvenience. Socially he starts as a pet/tool (parked, borrowed, ridden, the extra guard) and is promoted to family out loud at the midpoint. He ends the book as an actual combatant. His own read on himself never catches up.

## Evolution within this book

He arrives as comic furniture: parked in garages, borrowed for chores, hopping at the sight of people, apologizing for grooming a cat too hard. Mid-book the grabber upgrade makes him genuinely useful (hands, carrying, driving), and in the same stretch the ceiling scene turns him from a bit into a person: the fear of heights, the ocean, the conviction that the brave shark he came from was someone else, and an on-page promotion to family. After that he is trusted with a weapon and a heat, and in the final battle he is a real combatant, jumping through an arena with a flamethrower roaring and killing things with his mouth. Then he is smashed flat and ends the book legless, flopping, gathering his own broken pieces.

In hindsight the compulsive politeness stops reading as manners and starts reading as bother-aversion from someone who does not think he is entitled to be here. The apology-as-debt shape is literal — he believes he owes. And the physical fearlessness is, by his own account, not courage at all: he is brave only because he has never had time to look down.
