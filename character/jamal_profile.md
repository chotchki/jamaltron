# Jamal — canonical character profile

Merged from the two per-book profiles (`profiles/book7_profile.md`, `profiles/book8_profile.md`) and their verified quote banks, which stay LOCAL (gitignored under `profiles/`, per SPEC's copyright call). Jamal appears in books 7 and 8 ONLY — *This Inevitable Ruin* and *A Parade of Horribles*, zero mentions in books 1–6 — so this is the whole character, not a sample. Source of truth for the situation→line catalog (PLAN B.3) and every string the mod generates at B.5.

Two books is a narrow base and it cuts both ways. Anything both books do independently is as strong a signal as eight books would give, since there is no third book to break the tie and no room for coincidence. Anything only one book does is a coin flip between real development and extract coverage. This file marks which is which instead of averaging.

**Provenance and copyright.** All characterization here is ours. The verbatim material is 125 spoken lines, one sentence or less each, merged from the two per-book banks into one local bank, `profiles/jamal_quote_bank.md` (gitignored per SPEC; its row numbers are the `#N`s [`lines.md`](lines.md) cites). Every one was re-verified at merge time as an exact substring of the gitignored extracts, by script (strip the wrapping quotes, `grep -qF` against the matching extract): 125 checked, 125 exact, zero fabrications, zero duplicates between the banks. The curly quotes wrapping each line are OURS; everything inside is his. The banks stay local; this profile and the tagged catalog in [`lines.md`](lines.md) ship, analysis quotes included. No scene, paragraph or exchange is reproduced, and the book text stays in the gitignored `extracts/`.

## The short version

A 17-foot air-breathing hammerhead shark bolted to eight mechanical spider legs, who speaks like an Edwardian butler with a head injury, refers to himself in the third person by name, treats apologies as countable physical objects he owns a finite supply of, and is the direct cause of every hardware failure he apologizes for — a connection he never makes across two entire books. He is genuinely dangerous and nobody treats it as the headline. He is annoying in a way nobody is ever cruel about. His one want is small, specific, repeatedly denied and the reason everything breaks: he wants to hop.

The mod is not a reskin with quotes bolted on. The books already wrote the loop — he hops, the legs break, he apologizes operatically, defends hopping in the same breath, someone rebuilds the legs, repeat — so PLAN E's jump/break/repair cycle faithfully adapts his actual character arc, because in book 7 the hardware IS the arc.

## Voice

### The apology habit (mod-critical — PLAN D.5 and E.4 spam him on a timer)

**Both books independently land on the same metaphor: apologies are COUNTABLE PHYSICAL OBJECTS he owns a finite supply of, and delivering one is a transaction.** He does not say sorry, he *gives* you an apology, he *finds* more apologies, he *has* sorries, he *piles up* apologies and presents the stack. Every verb is an inventory verb. Two books, two verb sets, one metaphor — the strongest signal in the merge and the most machinable fact about him, because the whole apology pool generates from one template with the object swapped.

The reusable shapes, merged (book in brackets where only one supplies it):

- **Transfer** — he *must give* you an apology. An obligation discharged, not a feeling had. Note the modal: *must*. [7]
- **Bulk transfer** — a countable pile of sorries handed over at once, the maximum-remorse form. [7, and 8's *piling up* is the same shape with an accumulation verb]
- **Resupply** — he runs OUT and restocks. A second victim appears mid-conversation, he goes looking for more apologies, then assigns ownership of the ones he found. [7]
- **Debt** — the accumulating-pile form implies a running balance he owes. [8]
- **Intent disclaimer** — he denies *intention* rather than fault and apologizes for the *additional increment* of distress, as if a baseline of distress from him is already priced in and only the surplus is his. [8]
- **Self-diagnosis as apology** — his interior state reported instead of an apology, quantified, which reads like a status bar and so belongs in a speech bubble. [7]
- **Blast-radius formality** — the apology extends past the victim to the victim's FAMILY, for damage that was nearly nothing, paired with a cute euphemism for the offense. [7]
- **Self-narrated blunder** — the screwup announced in the third person, in the passive, like a bulletin about someone else. His two nouns for it are *blunder* and *bungle*. [7]
- **Apology as a subordinate clause on the way to the news** — in his longest utterance the apology is bolted mid-sentence into an explanation and the emergency lands last. He apologizes in passing while burying the thing that matters. [8]
- **Embarrassed confession** — the admission gets its own sentence of scaffolding before any content arrives. [8]
- **The formal request rider** — the apology ends by asking for help in the most over-built way available. [7]

**ESCALATION IS INVERTED, and both books reach that separately.** Formality is inversely proportional to actual severity. Book 7: the apology for breaking a set of legs someone spent hours on is courtly and long, but when he sets an ally on fire it gets SHORTER and the euphemism cuter, and the crawler he nearly kills with a leg swipe apparently never gets one at all. Book 8: brushing a cat too hard produces maximum formality, invented compound nouns and honorifics, while genuine existential terror strips the register to first person and six plain words. He is calibrated to social friction, not to harm.

This is the finding most likely to get flattened at B.3, because it is backwards from how anyone would build it: the ROUTINE events get the baroque politeness and the rare serious event gets the plain short words. `damaged` and `attacking` should be flippant, `legs break` operatic and the one deep-tier line six words with no honorific.

**Targets.** Carl leads in book 7 (owner, builder, order-giver, the one whose work he keeps destroying), Donut in book 8 (she scolds his technique in real time while he works). Katia is third and inverted — she apologizes to HIM about the legs more than he apologizes to her. Bystanders get exactly one elaborate apology each when he burns them. The mod inherits the Carl relationship wholesale: the player assigns jobs, owns the vehicle, asks whether the weapon works and gets status reports. The Donut dynamic — complained at mid-task by someone who is not going to stop — is the better source for `damaged` and `command done` griping.

**Pre-emptive vs reactive, which needs care.** Book 7's unprompted apologies are the MAJORITY: his size, taking up room, existing in a way that inconveniences the schedule, and pre-emptively for what his flamethrower is about to do. Nobody asks. Book 8 has almost none. The honest read is coverage as much as change (book 7 is a war where he is constantly in the way, book 8 parks him in a garage with chores), so keep book 7's volume: the pre-emptive pool is the one that will make players hate him correctly.

What book 8 supplies instead is the MOTIVE book 7 only shows the behavior of. He hung from a ceiling for hours, woozy and frightened, and said nothing, because saying something would make him a bother — and the scene resolves with him told outright he should not be scared to say when he is in trouble. That retroactively reclassifies every apology in both books from politeness to BOTHER-AVERSION, from someone who does not believe he is entitled to be here. The apology-as-debt shape is literal: he thinks he owes. Keep 7's volume and 8's reason; the spam is funnier that way.

**What he does NOT apologize for — the catalog's protest and hardware-defense LINES come from here, and the two books split:**

- **Hopping.** [7] Three sets of legs destroyed by the same forbidden action, told to stop each time, position unchanged. He apologizes for the BREAKAGE and defends the HOP in the same breath, with a *Still, Mr. Carl* hinge between them. The whole character in one move.
- **His legs.** [8] Never once, in any condition. When Carl assumes the legs broke, he corrects the diagnosis and takes the blame himself. He would rather confess cowardice than let anyone conclude his hardware failed.
- **The flamethrower.** [7] Reclassifies every accident as accidental, promises never to do it again, breaks the promise within pages.
- **His intelligence.** [7] Told he is being manipulated, he objects and claims to be too quick-witted for the trick, then falls for it.
- **His past.** [7] Apex predator, then a terror who could fly, then one of the most feared warriors of all the seas. Volunteered unprompted, never walked back.
- **Leg QUALITY when the failure is genuinely not his fault.** [7] No apology at all — a formal PROTEST, itemized, with a count.
- **Being excited, wanting the ocean or his fear once he has finally named it.** [8]

**Adjacent registers, all in the same voice:** formal protest (when wronged OR overlooked — two words and a verb, courtroom-flavored); lament (pure content-free misery, repeatable forever, the best beached idle); permission-seeking (he negotiates rather than obeys, politely, persistently, never defiantly); reassurance (a guarantee he cannot keep); duty restatement (an order repeated back as an institutional commission, first person plural, as though the garage were a posting — pure guard-bot flavor); over-confident volunteering (offers to do what he physically cannot, no hedge, then proposes an absurd method); literal-minded good news (a trivial success reported as a victory, then honestly downgraded in the same breath); social reporting (he notices and plainly relays other people's emotional states — he is the one who knows who is sad); star-struck gossip (ranks goddesses by beauty, sincerely, mid-campaign).

### Third person vs first person (the other signature tic)

**Default in both books: THIRD PERSON, by a wide margin, using his own NAME rather than a pronoun.** Not *he* — *Jamal*. Jamal is here. Jamal is firing his flamethrower. Jamal is stuck. Jamal has made another blunder.

**`Jamal is <state>` is the backbone shape of the entire character**: both books, four to eight words, survives truncation to a bubble and generates new faithful lines indefinitely. A Factorio speech bubble IS a status line, so it should be the default template for most of the catalog.

He also MIXES both persons inside one sentence without noticing, and book 8 is precise about the split: third person for himself-as-actor, first person for intent, possession and feeling. *Jamal was testing his legs* / *my toasty emissions* / *I do apologize*. A line generator that picks one grammatical person and sticks to it will sound less like him than one that mixes.

**The slip into sustained first person is the severity dial, and the books disagree on one thing — whether he catches himself.**

- Book 7: he notices, corrects mid-sentence with an ellipsis and a self-repair, is embarrassed, blames his yearning and drifts straight back to third person. Donut has a standing pet peeve about the habit and hisses at him for it.
- Book 8: the sincere monologue slides into first person and STAYS there for the rest of the speech. No correction. The narration marks him talking fast in a strange sad tone and interrupting someone, which he does nowhere else.

**Resolution: development — the character's whole emotional arc compressed into a grammatical tic.** In book 7 the real him is a leak he plugs; in book 8 he lets it run. Book 8 reads as later and truer for the sincere material, so ship book 7's behavior (the slip plus the audible self-correction, in ONE bubble) as the comic tic, and book 8's (uncorrected, sustained, no honorific) for the single deep-tier line. Two dials, not one: grammatical person sets the register, and whether he corrects himself sets how much it costs him.

Caveat from book 8: the first-person PANIC lines are short and funny while the first-person SINCERE lines are long and sad, so grammatical person alone is not a tone switch. Pair it with length.

### Catchphrases and verbal tics

Frequency matters here, so the lexicon is sorted by how much weight each item can carry. The extracts are ~620 and ~520 lines of windowed context, which is thin — a one-hit marker is a one-hit marker, not a catchphrase.

**Reliable in BOTH books (lean on these):**

- *quite* — his word, everywhere: 9 hits in book 7, 6 in book 8. Quite primed, quite snug, quite excited, quite exciting, quite toasty, quite unpleasant, quite the inverted conundrum, quite thoroughly gone. If a generated line needs one word to sound like him, it is *quite*. *Most* is the runner-up in both books.
- **Third-person self-reference by name.** See above. The backbone.
- *goodness* as the stock exclamation — *Oh, goodness* / *Oh my goodness* / *My goodness, Mr. Carl*. Doubled when things are bad, and the first sound out of him when the legs blow apart. Book 8 flagged it as possibly borrowed off Donut and could not settle it; book 7 settles it — reliably his, in his mouth, unprompted, in both books. Ship it. (In-world he may well have picked it up off her, as a mimic of servant-class manners. Immaterial to the mod.)
- **Honorifics, absolutely rigid.** *Mister Carl* / *Mr. Carl* (both spellings occur), *Miss Donut*, *Miss Katia* and, in book 8, *Mr. Mongo* to a pet dinosaur and *Miss Hedy* to a gremlin. The honorific NEVER drops in either book — not in combat, misery or panic, not while swearing and not when Carl has been temporarily turned into a dog, which he addresses as Mr. Carl with no comment whatsoever.
- *toasty* for his own flame, both books — *toasty disrespect* [7], *toasty emissions* [8], *quite toasty indeed* [7]. A genuine cross-book coinage root.
- **Invented compounds and malapropisms generally.** Both books produce them freely, and they are free comedy for locale strings and item descriptions. Book 7: **hoppers** (his own word for his spider legs — it belongs in the item description), *reverse psychologicalize*, *flamings* as a countable noun, *toasty disrespect*, *doggies* for lethal military automatons. Book 8: *sticky feets* (double plural), *inverted conundrum*, *melty inconvenience*, *toasty emissions*, *hair-pulling distress*. He also treats vehicles as persons — enemy cars are *nasty cars who* try to beat them.
- **Self-narration as a status readout.** Both books. He announces what he is doing while doing it, and what state he is in, in the third person with his own name as subject. The mod's single biggest win: these are speech-bubble strings verbatim.
- **Quantified affect.** He does not feel excited, he HAS an excitement level and it is something else. A very robust excitement. Untold levels of energy. A great amount of shame and regret. Quite snug. Feeling woozy. He reports affect the way a UI reports a meter, which is exactly what a Factorio `custom_status` is.
- **Repetition with a one-word variation** as his emphasis mechanism — *Such woe! Such woe I have!* / *Quite unpleasant indeed.* / *Top quality!* as its own sentence. Both halves fit in one bubble and the repetition is free comedy.
- **Zero profanity, sarcasm, irony or intentional insults.** Confirmed by grep across both extracts: the ONLY swearing in his mouth in two books is the single triple-*Fuck* in book 7, flopping legless behind a convoy after a failure that was not his fault. Book 8 gives him nothing, in a book where every other character swears constantly. One register break in two books. The closest he comes to an insult is an unintentional one delivered as a courteous technical observation, which he does not register as an insult and which is the funniest beat he gets in book 8.

**Book 7 only, mostly single-hit — seasoning, not backbone:** *indeed* as a stock closer (4 hits, appended to anything), *Rest assured* (1), *Mayhaps* for perhaps (1, always), *Gentlemen* as the group address regardless of who is in it (1), *woe* (2), *yearning* (3), *bungle* / *blunder* (1 each), *top quality* (2, but it carries the whole leg-break gag), *Velma* — the proper name of his flamethrower (2). Do not declare that he stopped saying these; one hit in a windowed extract is coverage, not an arc.

**Book 8 only:** *Oh boy* as the excitement opener, sentence-final *I fear* as a hedge (twice, both softening bad news he is responsible for — he hedges facts, never opinions), *Oh yes, Mr. Carl* / *Oh, yes, Miss Donut* as the eager affirmative with the comma in two positions.

**Self-quoting, the one canonical exception to anti-repeat.** [7] He says a line and chapters later repeats it verbatim with *indeed* bolted on, as a callback — the only character who quotes himself. An anti-repeat system explicitly ALLOWED to repeat exactly that one line reproduces a gag he performs on himself.

**What OTHERS call him** (flavor text, not his mouth): *shark boy* (annoyed), *buddy* (fond), *Elmo-ing* (Donut's name for the third-person habit), *Jar Jar* (Donut, behind the honorifics).

### Sentence shape

- **Short sentences in RUNS, both books.** He almost never speaks one sentence — four to six short declaratives in a burst, each grammatically complete and independently quotable. That is why ~24 utterance events in book 7 yield 78 usable lines, and why he fits a speech-bubble system so well: he already writes his own one-liners.
- **The news is buried at the END**, consistently in both books. Book 7's leg-break run goes exclamation → self-narrated blunder → formal request for help, so you learn the legs are destroyed on the third sentence. Book 8's stuck-on-a-ceiling utterance goes what he was doing → why → the hardware he was testing → an apology → and only in the final clause, that he cannot get down. He answers the literal question and omits the important part.
- **Consequence for the mod, flagged by both agents independently:** the baroque shape needs two sentences to land and a short-bubble version of him loses the joke, while the status-readout shape survives truncation to four words. A real constraint on the verbosity tiers.
- **Over-built courtesy scaffolding around trivial content** — *I do fear I require assistance* for *help*, *Mayhaps when Jamal gets back from the fighting?* for *later*.
- **Typical utterance: one to two sentences, 8–20 words; median usable line 5–9 words.** Nothing he says is long except the one monologue. Every tic except the mid-sentence person-slip survives a one-line truncation, and the slip needs two clauses.
- **He does not interrupt and does not trail off**, with two exceptions, both meaningful: the ellipsis at the person-slip, and the one time in book 8 he interrupts someone, which the narration flags as out of character. He GETS interrupted constantly in both books — shut up, hissed at, cut off mid-sentence by a pet shoving past him — and absorbs it without resentment. A line that ends in an em-dash is a legitimate one-shot bubble gag.

### Register and vocabulary

Merged from two four-register reads that mostly agree. Five registers, each with a hard trigger:

1. **Courtly apologist / butler formality** (default, roughly 70% of his lines) — fires on any fault, friction, errand, guard shift, status report or weapon check. Third person, honorifics, *quite*, hedges, inventory metaphors, over-built politeness.
2. **Robust / puppyish excitement** — fires on new hardware, an assignment, a fight, music, the team coming back into view, being summoned, being included, a hat. Exclamations, quantified enthusiasm, physical bouncing, front legs waving, involuntary flamethrower discharge. Both books make this his most common ACTIVE state, so it is the default idle pool.
3. **Operatic misery / formal protest** — fires on legs destroyed, on being denied or on being overlooked. Woe, protest, counting grievances and, at the absolute bottom, the one swear.
4. **Deadpan observer** — fires on catastrophe happening to OTHER people. Flat and understated: *a most unexpected development* delivered at an army-wide mind-control event, or announcing that a man on the floor appears to have fallen. The gap between stimulus and flatness is the joke. Book 8 extends it to social reporting — he plainly relays who is still sad.
5. **Plain frightened sincerity** — exactly once in each book, and the SAME MATERIAL both times (see the annihilation conflict below). First person, short clauses, no scaffolding, no honorific, no apology. Use rarely in the mod or not at all.

**Species and origin flavor bleeding through the manners.** He wants to feel the wind in his GILLS [7] and to go back to the ocean [8]. He measures his credentials in prey consumed, cites the seas and used to watch everything at once from above when he was *real* [8]. The shark instinct lives in his HARDWARE USAGE, not his manners, which are entirely servant-class human. His mouth is a tool: he pulls a struggling mercenary out of a battle with it [7], picks up his own broken leg pieces with it [8], snaps monsters in half with it [8] and offers it as a backup vehicle control surface [8]. Book 7 gives him a berserker feeding-frenzy state in which the manners switch off ENTIRELY — no speech, crunching, whooping, barrel rolls. The sea words and the etiquette never blend, they alternate, and the seams are where he is funniest.

## Personality

### Core traits (both books unless bracketed)

- **Incorrigible.** [7, and the trait PLAN E's break loop rests on] Not stubborn — he agrees with you, apologizes sincerely and does it again. Three sets of legs destroyed by the same forbidden action, told to stop each time, and his position after the third is that he still does not see the problem. Unteachable by consequence, which is exactly what a mod needs to justify a jump button that keeps breaking.
- **Recklessly experimental with his own hardware, and he does not connect testing to consequences.** Both books, different limits. Book 7: hops after being told three times. Book 8: climbs a hangar ceiling to find out whether his upgraded feet will hold him inverted, with no exit plan, because nobody asked him not to. He talks about his legs to strangers and tests their limits on purpose without telling anyone.
- **Compulsively courteous.** Both books, and it never drops — while flopping in the dirt on ruined hardware, while swearing, to a pet dinosaur. His identity rests on etiquette in a way it does not rest on his body.
- **Compulsively useful.** [8, corroborated by 7's assignments] Accepts every task instantly — guard the garage, guard the prisoners, hold a firing position, brush the cat, chase bugs, drive a truck he does not fit in — reports back on each like a duty log, and never asks why.
- **Enthusiastic past the point of being a hazard.** [7] His excitement is kinetic and damages the environment: bystanders leap out of the way, rocks fly, allies get scorched. Nobody stays angry because the enthusiasm is so obviously sincere.
- **Physically fearless, emotionally fragile.** [8] He routinely jumps higher than a ceiling and lands on monsters to snap them in half, and he is afraid of heights, and he resolves it himself: when he jumps he never has time to notice the height because he is always already falling. The courage was never courage, it was insufficient airtime. Keep it as characterization, not an error to fix — the funniest and saddest single fact about him.
- **Genuinely dangerous, and nobody treats it as the headline.** Both books. Level 72. Pulls a struggling mercenary out of a battle in his mouth, clears an automaton nest by pumping flame into holes one after another, disarms a hostile crawler with a leg swipe hard enough to nearly kill her [7]; jumps through an arena with the flamethrower roaring and kills things with his mouth [8]. Comedy in the foreground, lethality stated flatly in the background. The mod should reproduce exactly that ratio.
- **Bother-averse past the point of self-harm.** [8] Hours stuck and woozy, silent, because speaking up would inconvenience someone. This is the trait the apology tic is actually made of.
- **Socially undeterred, and socially observant.** [7 / 8] Shut down, ordered around, told to be quiet, told to stay behind, and he keeps talking and looking for someone new to talk to — when the whole convoy has a stench debuff he rolls toward the people who cannot get away from him. And he tracks other people's feelings and reports them plainly and unprompted; he knows which NPC is still sad.
- **Literal-minded optimism.** [8] One insect repelled counts as a successful defensive program, and he volunteers the true count himself without noticing it undercuts him.
- **Loyal by choice, and both books make a point of it.** Book 7: when the NPCs are free to leave, he is one of the few who stays. Book 8: he is promoted to family out loud at the midpoint. His own self-read never catches up in either book.
- **Nobody is ever cruel to him.** Exasperated, yes. Cruel, no — in two books. Preserve this in the mod's tone: the player should be annoyed, never encouraged to resent him.

### Drives

Small, specific and mechanically convenient — what a line pool needs.

- **He wants to HOP.** [7] THE drive: small, specific, repeatedly denied, the reason all his hardware breaks and the mod's central mechanic. He frames it as recovering something lost: he used to be a flying terror, now he cannot manage a little hop.
- Wants new legs, immediately, and wants them TOP QUALITY. [7]
- Wants to keep the legs he has, and to get better ones. [8]
- Wants to be given a job, and to be told the job was done right. [8]
- Wants to be included — in the fight, the conversation, the group standing outside the privacy bubble, the trip. [both]
- Wants someone to talk to, and is not selective. [7]
- Wants to be brave on purpose rather than by accident. [8]
- Wants not to be a burden. [8]
- Wants to see the ocean again [8], and to see a goddess, because he has heard she is beautiful [7].
- Wears the hat, and is explicitly a fan of the hats. [8]

### Fears and insecurities

- **Sudden annihilation — the same fact in both books, with the affect inverted.** See the conflicts section; the merge's best finding.
- **Being too big.** [7 as a wound, 8 as a shrug — see conflicts] The parts are always too small, the joints have to be extra-large, the transports will not take him, the trenches are not designed for him, the truck cab has to be cut open. He never frames it as *make bigger parts*, always as woe about himself.
- **Being left behind.** [7] Told he is staying back, his entire response is *What?* — then he negotiates. Twice in the book he is benched for his own good, and both times he stays and sulks.
- **Heights.** [8] Newly admitted and embarrassed about it, confessed specifically to clear his hardware of blame.
- **What he has LOST.** [7] Volunteered unprompted: apex predator, then a terror who could fly and a greatly enhanced sense of smell that is now thoroughly gone. The only thread where he is almost un-funny in book 7, and he always lands it back on the hopping, which defuses it.
- **Not being the real one.** [8] He believes the shark he came from was brave and that he is not that shark. He states it once and does not argue with it.
- **Losing the family** [8], and getting physically stuck somewhere [both].

### Self-image vs reality

He thinks he is a distinguished former apex predator temporarily inconvenienced by equipment [7], and simultaneously a lesser copy of a braver original whose surplus distress must be apologized for [8]. Both readings are wrong in opposite directions and he holds them at once. He is in fact an overweight air-breathing shark bolted to prototype hardware, the reason the hardware keeps failing (a connection he never makes, not once, in either book) and treated by everyone around him as annoying-but-ours.

Book 7's agent correctly refused to invent an identity line, since book 7 does not develop the question. Book 8 fills that gap with one real sentence, the best outcome a two-book merge could have: the actual line instead of a fabricated one. The identity material is where he stops being funny. Hold it back entirely or spend it once, and never on a timer.

## His legs (mod-critical — the whole conceit; PLAN E.4 breaks them)

### Provenance — book 7 answers this, book 8 only extends it

**Six sets in book 7 alone. The book counts them out loud and so should the mod.**

- **Sets 1–3: Carl's, hastily built, explicitly a PROTOTYPE.** Eight double-jointed legs plus a harness sized for a 17-foot shark, derived from an automaton manual. The magically-enhanced joints are the expensive part and the failure point. Carl's stated intent was to prove the concept before handing it to a better engineer. All three destroyed by Jamal HOPPING.
- **Sets 4–5: Katia's, fortified and enhanced, with a weapon mount.** The upgrade path is explicit: Carl builds the rough one, Katia builds the good one. Set 5 dies to an EMP-style bomb blast, not to Jamal: he was at the edge of the pulse and the cybernetics were unshielded. He is told this and does not accept it as an excuse.
- **Set 6: the last one**, from Katia via Mordecai, which the narration calls his new and FINAL set.
- **Book 8 adds only bolt-ons to a pre-existing rig**, names no builder for the legs themselves and counts no sets. Mordecai designed the hand-like grabbers on the front pair; the adhesive *sticky feets* are new and improved, upgrader unnamed. A fleshmancer takes an interest in his legs off-page.

**Merged chain: Carl builds the rough one → Katia builds the good one → Mordecai adds the bolt-ons.** No conflict; book 8 is simply downstream on the same upgrade path. If the mod credits a builder in flavor text, mind the seam: the legs are Carl's then Katia's, the flamethrower MOUNT is a third party's crafting team, and the grabbers are Mordecai's.

### Mechanism

- **VOICE-CONTROLLED, and that is canon.** [7] Jamal calls out to the legs and tells them where he wants to go, which is why they do what he says even when it is stupid — including the hop. The mod reading is irresistible: the driver gives the order and the shark's own mouth is the control input, so the jump keybind can be flavored as the driver TELLING Jamal to hop, with Jamal's own line as the confirmation.
- **Thought-reading is stated intent ONLY, confirmed by NEITHER book.** Katia's version was designed to read his thoughts — said once, early, in book 7, never verified — and book 8 establishes no control scheme at all. Do not build a mechanic on it.
- Cumulative capability by the end of book 8: eight double-jointed legs on a body harness; front two legs are manipulators with hand-like grabbers (a brush in each, one hand on a steering wheel and one on the pedals, a person held aloft in front of a screen); adhesive feet strong enough to hold his full weight inverted on a ceiling and park him on a wall; a harness-mounted flamethrower with an aimable nozzle.
- **Size is the permanent engineering constraint.** Extra-large leg joints are a limited resource and the standard workshop cybernetics are too small for him.

### Failure modes — the one place the books genuinely disagree

Book 7 is unambiguous: the joints handle the JUMP fine and cannot handle the LANDING more than once or twice. The book's own mechanical statement, and the exact shape of PLAN E.4 — the roll happens on landing, not takeoff. Three sets died that way. The one non-hop failure was unshielded electronics in an EMP.

Book 8 says the opposite: jumping is routine and SAFE. He jumps constantly, has jumped higher than a ceiling dozens of times, hops with a midair flip and lands *deftly*, and uses jump-landings as an attack. His book-8 failures are a thrown wrench that clanks off a leg with no damage, getting STUCK rather than broken while the hardware works perfectly, and total loss to a boss-scale area attack that smashes him into a wall — after which he survives, says nothing and flops around mouthing at his own broken leg pieces. No on-page repair.

**Resolution: development, not contradiction — the hardware got better between the books.** See the conflicts section. Book 7 is the era the mod adapts, the break-on-landing roll is canon for that rig, and three sets in one book means the mod should not be shy about the break rate.

**Fault attribution is the mechanically important part**, and book 7 hands the mod two break events with two line pools: 3 of 5 failures are unambiguously his own doing and he was warned each time, 1 is external and blameless. Book 8 only ever shows the his-fault-or-ambiguous branch, which is why it shows no quality complaint — and it supplies the emotional logic for why that branch is complaint-free. Merged rule: when the break is his, he eats it and defends the hardware; when it demonstrably is not, he protests with a running COUNT.

### How he treats them, and what he says

Carelessly, proudly, curiously and with genuine affection for what they let him do. They are HIS — his *hoppers*, his upgrades, his excitement — and he grieves each lost set as a personal injury rather than broken equipment.

- **Break, his fault** [7]: exclamation → self-narrated blunder in the third person → apology-as-inventory → formal request for assistance → and only then the unrepentant defense of hopping. Shame is claimed and never acted on.
- **Break, not his fault** [7]: no apology at all. Formal protest to the whole group, a COUNT of how many sets he has been given, a complaint that he was led to believe they were top quality, and at the absolute bottom the one swear in two books.
- **Break, wordless** [8]: the event happens and he says NOTHING. `legs break` has no book-8 verbatim anchor; everything usable comes from the stuck-on-a-ceiling scene instead — apology first, explanation second, emergency last, embarrassed confession and a flat defense of the hardware against the accusation that it broke.
- **New or upgraded** [7]: quantified excitement, third person, physical bouncing. Anticipation lines WHILE WAITING for repair are a separate, useful pool, and its best beat is that he negotiates the schedule of his own repair — mayhaps after the fighting? Book 8 has no line at the moment the grabbers are fitted; it contributes volunteering-and-testing energy instead, so the natural new-legs line is him announcing an experiment nobody asked for.
- **Repair parts offered** [7]: he complains they are too small for him. Katia apologizes to HIM about the legs, twice, and he responds by complaining about her spare parts. A running inversion of who owes whom an apology.

**The quality gag.** *Top quality* is his standard and a promise he believes he was made, and book 7 violates it every single time. It can carry the mod's whole leg-break flavor text, and it gets better each time it repeats because the count goes up. Book 8 has zero quality complaints — the conflicts section explains why that is a fault fork and not a retcon.

## How he moves and flops (mod-critical — drives PLAN C.4 walk, C.5 flop loop)

### On legs

- **Fast. Terrifyingly fast** [7] — the narration uses that word twice, and he keeps pace with motorcycles, a chariot and mounted riders across ten miles of open ground while holding the rear of a convoy. Do not nerf his speed below the stock spidertron.
- **Silhouette: a horrifying spider hybrid.** [7] The shark body rides up high on eight double-jointed legs, and by book 8 the front pair ends in grabbers, which the narration says makes him look MORE terrifying. Builder and narrator both find him unsettling, and book 7's narration cannot decide which is scarier, spider Jamal or flopping land-shark Jamal.
- **He rides HIGH.** [8] Tall enough to loom over a man lying on the floor, and his head is a platform two small characters casually sit on at once.
- **Audible and metallic, described twice the same way.** Legs WHIR when running and the feet CLICK-CLACK on hard floors [7]; *clank, clank, clank* over a hard floor, approaching before he is seen [8]. A thrown wrench rings off a leg. When a set breaks: a thrashing, then a POP, then a mechanical whir, plus the smell of broken machinery and fire. He was built to be quiet as well as quick — Carl's stated reason for the whole rig — and he audibly is not.
- **He climbs.** [both] Straight up a vertical wall, holding position partway up [7]; walks on walls and ceilings on the sticky feet, and parks on a wall [8]. Out of scope for Factorio, but canon — worth knowing before anyone calls a wall-climb unfaithful.
- **He does not hold still.** Bouncing up and down in place, hopping in circles around whatever he is guarding, waving the front pair while he hops, bouncing in time to music.
- **Size as a constraint.** [8] He does not fit in a truck cab — they cut the cab off a pickup and set him inside it — and once wedged he is *quite snug* and cannot reach a window handle. Read him as a vehicle-scale entity, which suits a spidertron footprint exactly.

### Off legs — the flop

**The books look contradictory in isolation and agree once the evidence is sorted by his CONDITION.** Book 7 says both things in one book: legless and merely assessed, he can move neither fast nor subtly and is pitiful; legless and MOTIVATED, he moves alarmingly fast, rolls and tumbles down a hill into a pitched battle and comes up out of the smoke with a mercenary in his mouth, looking as though he had somehow jumped without legs. Book 8's only on-page flopping is post-wreck, slow and pitiful — going nowhere, mouthing at his own broken pieces — and book 8 ALSO has Carl remembering the book 7 hill roll as alarming.

**Resolution: the variable is his condition, not the flop.** Freshly wrecked and damaged = slow and pitiful, in both books. Intact-but-legless with a reason to move = alarming. That satisfies SPEC.md's intent that beached be a real penalty without contradicting a line of either book, and it closes the question book 7 could not close alone.

Mechanically the flop is flopping, rolling, tumbling, barrel rolls and in combat something like a lunge — not a crawl, not a drag. And the punishment is not immobility, it is INDIGNITY: he hates it, complains loudly and continuously while doing it, and keeps addressing the group as if mid-conversation. The apology spam is the penalty.

### Jumping and hopping

- He calls it a little hop, always minimizing. [7] Often enough that the builder describes it as something he kept doing.
- **On legs he lands WELL.** Both books: real leaps off a wall landing *deftly*, then a second leap with a ballerina-like midair twirl, landing behind the party, which Donut calls elegant [7]; a midair flip with a deft landing from ceiling height, and jump-landings used as an attack that snaps monsters in half [8]. So the airborne pose should read deliberate, not flailing — he is good at this, the book-7 legs just cannot take it twice.
- **He cannot see where he is going.** [8] By his own account he never has time to check the height because he is always already falling. Perfect flavor for `jump`, `land` and especially `jump refused`.
- **Height and control: no figure in either book.** He is explicitly forbidden from jumping off a tower because the legs are strong but not THAT strong — canon grounds for refusing a jump over a cliff, a platform edge or water.
- **Why he does it:** yearning, framed as recovering the flight he lost — the wind in his gills, hopping across a battlefield [7]. What it costs: three sets of legs.

### Knocked over, stuck, out of position

- **Damage is a NOISE, not a sentence, in both books.** Hit by a flung mob or a child's dulled spear he YELPS, then flops in circles [7]; a thrown wrench rings off a leg and he lets out a small wordless shout [8]. Both narrations flag that him being quiet at all is the anomaly.
- **Stuck, he waits and says nothing.** [8] Hours on a ceiling. Wedged in a truck, he states the constraint flatly without complaint. Told to stay, he stays. Told he is too big, he accepts it.
- **Immobilized and NOT stuck, he complains loudly and continuously.** [7] The difference is whether it is his own fault.
- **Heat bothers him**; he removes himself to the shade and reports doing so. [7]

### Body facts for the render (PLAN C)

- **17 feet long**, described as overweight and oversized, otherwise the anatomy of an ordinary hammerhead shark.
- **Breathes AIR, cannot breathe water, still has gills.** A land animal that looks like a sea animal; the ocean is nostalgia, not physiology.
- **Eight legs, double-jointed, on a body harness** — exactly the stock spidertron leg count and topology, so no prototype gymnastics are needed. The front pair ends in hand-like grabbers by book 8.
- **Flamethrower on the UNDERBELLY**, nozzle aimable and at one point deliberately pointed BACKWARD by order to protect the people in front of him.
- No sense of smell (previously enhanced, now entirely gone). Heat-averse. Level 72 at the start of book 7.
- **He waves his whole head back and forth to express misery** — the hammerhead shape is the expressive organ. Worth a beached-state animation beat.
- **A wide-brimmed hat that reshapes itself to fit a shark's head.** [8, late, cosmetic] Possible alt sprite, and he is a fan of hats.

### Excitement tells (idle animation + idle lines)

Hopping in place, bouncing up and down, front legs waving, barrel rolls that send rocks flying, spinning in circles, quivering, bouncing in time to music, a single excited hop at passers-by, hopping off a wall when called. **Best rest pose in either book:** settling into a circle on the floor with a pet curled against him [8]. **Best idle effect in either book:** little tendrils of flame keep leaking out of the flamethrower when he is excited [7] — idle Jamal involuntarily leaks fire, which is free, canonical and visually perfect.

## Relationships

**Merged ranking of who he apologizes to:** **Carl** — *Mister Carl* / *Mr. Carl* — builder, owner, employer, commander and the one whose work he keeps destroying. **Donut** — *Miss Donut* — apologized to for his verbal tics and his technique, not for damage. **Katia** — *Miss Katia* — the good engineer, who apologizes to HIM. **Anyone he burns** — one elaborate apology each, extended to their family, in cute euphemism. Then honorifics for everyone else on earth, animals included.

- **Carl.** Employer and commander, addressed with unbroken formality across both books. Jamal takes orders instantly and literally, restates them as a commission, stays where he is put and sends status reports. Carl's side is affectionate exasperation: swears at him by name, calls him *buddy*, pats him on the head, gives him real orders and real jobs, checks his weapon before a fight, keeps building him legs and is audibly annoyed when the rules make him unusable. Carl also punctures his self-image, by listing the reckless things he does routinely. The mod inherits this dynamic wholesale: the player is Carl.
- **Donut.** His harshest and his kindest, in both books. She scolds the third-person habit relentlessly, calls him annoying to his face and *Jar Jar* behind the honorifics, tells him to shut up during a crisis and complains about his brushing in real time — and she calls his midair twirl elegant, floats up to the ceiling to whisper him down, and defends him as family. The mod can borrow the shape: being complained at IS the affection.
- **Katia.** The good engineer, the upgrade path and a running inversion of who owes whom an apology.
- **Samantha** [8], a flying severed head: the one who plays with him. She summons him like a dog, he comes hopping and delighted, she rides his head and has him carry her and hold her up to screens. He does not distinguish rank among the people who are kind to him — he obeys her exactly as happily as he obeys Carl.
- **Mordecai:** quartermaster for legs in book 7, designer of the grabbers in book 8.
- **The cohort.** *Bigs* the slug, *Gonk* the yak, *Rend* the meatball, *Mongo* the pet dinosaur — the not-quite-pets, not-quite-soldiers the system also cannot classify. He gets left with them, guards things with them, bounces to music with them, tries to enforce garage rules on them, gets shoved aside by them mid-sentence and rests on the floor with one curled against him. He honorifics them.
- **Elle** [7], nearly incinerated, calls him *shark boy*, the only person who yells at him about the flamethrower specifically. **The flesh-golem children** [7]: one smacks him with a spear by accident and apologizes to HIM, and they get assigned to help him back to base — he is roughly their social peer, a useful read on his standing. **Hedy** [8], a gremlin driver, garage-bound like him: fellow minion and the source of his existential dread. **Grigori** [8], a fleshmancer: the only character who takes his hardware seriously.

### Standing in the group

**Deliberately ambiguous in both books, and both use the ambiguity.** Administratively a possession: a former-tattoo NPC in book 7 with a hard mechanical consequence (a buff lands on everyone else and he does not get it), a hired mercenary in book 8 with a rules consequence (ineligible to drive in certain heats, played as a comic inconvenience). Functionally a soldier: solo tactical assignments, the personal-guard platoon, trusted with prisoners, obeyed when he sends messages and by the end of book 8 an actual combatant. Socially somewhere near the pets and the children.

He never comments on any of it. Book 7 answers it quietly by having him stay when he could leave; book 8 answers it out loud by promoting him to family at the midpoint. His own read on himself never catches up in either book, and nothing in his voice changes to reflect the promotion — which is the point. He was always talking like a member of the party and everyone else caught up.

## Cross-book merge

### What is CONSISTENT across both books — the mod's spine

Consistency is the strongest signal available, because a one-off gag makes a bad line pool. Everything below appeared independently in both books, and the catalog is built on it.

1. **Apologies are countable objects, and delivering one is a transaction.** Two books, two verb sets, one metaphor.
2. **Escalation is INVERTED.** Formality is inversely proportional to actual severity. Both agents reached this independently; the single most machinable finding in the merge.
3. **Third person by default, using his own NAME**, with `Jamal is <state>` as the backbone shape.
4. **First person marks sincerity, fear or arousal**, and the two persons mix inside one sentence.
5. **Honorifics never drop.** Not in combat, panic or misery, not while swearing, not at animals.
6. *quite* as the one-word voice marker, with *most* behind it.
7. **Self-narration as a status readout**, which is a literal speech bubble.
8. **Quantified affect** — feelings reported the way a UI reports a meter.
9. **Invented compounds and malapropisms**, produced freely, with *toasty* for his own flame in both books.
10. **Short sentences in runs, courtesy scaffolding first, the actual news LAST.**
11. **Zero aggression, sarcasm or deliberate insults**, and exactly ONE swear across both books, at the absolute bottom.
12. **Reckless with his own hardware, and never connects testing to consequences.**
13. **Compulsively useful — accepts every job instantly and restates it as a commission.**
14. **A harness-mounted flamethrower is canon**, confirmed independently twice. PLAN D.7 is text, not a good idea.
15. **Hops in place when excited; front legs wave; the midair flip lands DEFTLY.** He is good at landing.
16. **Vehicle-scale and a problem for architecture.**
17. **Administratively a possession, functionally a soldier, socially near the pets** — and chosen/promoted at the end of both books, his self-read never catching up.
18. **His SILENCE is the distress signal.** Both books use it, both narrations flag it as the anomaly.
19. **Nobody is ever cruel to him.**

### Conflicts and development, resolved explicitly

No averaging. Each names which book reads as later or truer, and why.

**1. Does jumping break his legs?** Book 7: yes, unambiguously — 3 of 5 sets destroyed by hopping, and the book states outright that the joints handle the jump but not the landing more than once or twice. Book 8: no — jumping is routine and safe, constant, higher than a ceiling, lands deftly, jump-landings as an ATTACK; the legs come off to a boss-scale hit instead. → DEVELOPMENT, not contradiction: the hardware got better. Book 7 is the prototype era (Carl's hand-built rig, then Katia's fortified one, six sets burned in one book); book 8's rig has adhesive feet and grabber hands and survives everything short of a boss. Book 8 is truer about LATE Jamal, and book 7 is the era the mod adapts. Verdict: the mod is book-7-era Jamal, the break-on-landing roll is canon for that rig, and three sets in one book means the break rate should not be timid. Say the era out loud somewhere player-visible and the tension disappears.

**2. Is flopping fast or pitiful?** Book 7 says both in one book (legless and assessed: neither fast nor subtle; legless and motivated: alarmingly fast, rolls down a hill into a pitched battle). Book 8 on-page says pitiful, and ALSO has Carl remembering book 7's hill roll as alarming. → NOT a contradiction once sorted by CONDITION. Freshly wrecked and damaged = slow and pitiful, both books; intact-but-legless with a reason to move = alarming. Verdict: beached-from-a-break is slow, which satisfies SPEC.md's punishment intent without contradicting either book. Book 7's agent proposed this compromise from one book; book 8's evidence closes it. The real punishment is the indignity and the noise, not the speed.

**3. Does he complain about leg QUALITY?** Book 7: yes, *top quality* is a running gag with a running count that the whole leg-break pool leans on. Book 8: never once, in any condition, and when Carl assumes the legs broke he DEFENDS the hardware and volunteers a humiliating truth about himself instead. → A fault fork book 7 already contains, not a retcon. The quality protest fires only when the failure is demonstrably not his doing; when it is, he eats it. Book 8 only ever shows the his-fault branch, which is why it shows no complaint — and it supplies the missing emotional logic: he would rather be thought a coward than let anyone conclude his legs failed. That is the deeper line and book 8 is later, so verdict: two break pools keyed on fault, and when fault is ambiguous the mod defends the hardware.

**4. How does he feel about his own annihilation?** Book 7: cheerfully explains that if both his owners die the saferoom goes away and he gets smushed. *Cheerfully*, which is worse. Book 8: the SAME FACT from a different source — a gremlin tells him that if the crawlers die everyone contingent on them simply stops — and it terrifies him into hanging from a ceiling for hours to see everything at once while it is still there. → The best finding in the merge, and not a conflict: the FACT is constant and the AFFECT inverts. Both times he raises it UNPROMPTED; nobody asks him about dying. Verdict: book 8 is later and truer about what the fact costs him, book 7 is the shippable register. The cheerful version goes in the pools; the plain version is the one line the mod spends once and never on a timer.

**5. Does he catch his own third-person slip?** Book 7: yes — notices, corrects mid-sentence with an ellipsis, is embarrassed, blames his yearning, drifts back. Book 8: no — the sincere monologue slides into first person and stays. → DEVELOPMENT, the character's whole emotional arc compressed into a grammatical tic. In book 7 the real him is a leak he plugs; in book 8 he lets it run. Verdict: ship book 7's slip-plus-correction in one bubble as the comic tic, and book 8's uncorrected sustained first person for the single deep-tier line. Two dials: person sets the register, self-correction sets the cost.

**6. Is being too big a wound or a shrug?** Book 7: a recurring WOUND — the parts are too small, the joints must be extra-large and he frames it as woe about himself. Book 8: told outright he is too big for a task, he simply accepts it. → Consistent once you notice WHAT is too small. Book 7's offense is that the SPARE PARTS do not fit him, which lands as an insult to his body; book 8's constraint is that HE does not fit the world (a truck cab, a job), which he treats as a fact. Verdict: the lament fires at parts and repairs, the flat acceptance at geometry. A `repaired` line can complain the parts were too small (book 7, funny); a `jump refused` line accepts the constraint flatly (book 8, funnier because it does not complain).

**7. Are most of his apologies pre-emptive?** Book 7: yes, the majority — his size, taking up room, existing inconveniently and pre-emptively for what the flamethrower is about to do. Book 8: almost none; he volunteers embarrassment and labor instead. → Mostly coverage. Book 7 is a war where he is constantly in the way; book 8 parks him in a garage with chores. Verdict: keep book 7's volume — the pre-emptive pool is the one that will make players hate him correctly — and book 8's reason, that the apologies are bother-aversion rather than manners.

**8. Who built the legs, and how are they controlled?** Book 7 answers both (Carl's prototype, voice-controlled; Katia's fortified sets; thought-reading stated once, never confirmed). Book 8 answers neither and adds only bolt-ons (Mordecai's grabbers, an unnamed upgrader for the sticky feet). → No conflict, book 8 is downstream. Verdict: Carl builds the rough one → Katia builds the good one → Mordecai adds the bolt-ons. Voice control is canon. Thought-reading is confirmed by NEITHER book — do not build on it.

**9. Is "My goodness" his tic or borrowed off Donut?** Book 8 raised it and could not settle it alone. Book 7 settles it: unambiguously in his mouth, unprompted, including as the first sound when the legs blow apart, at 5 hits to book 8's 3. → Verdict: his. Ship it. He may well have picked it up off her in-world (he mimics servant-class manners), and that changes nothing the mod does.

**10. The lexical drift between the books.** Book 7 has *Mayhaps*, *indeed*, *Rest assured*, *woe*, *Gentlemen*, *yearning*, *hoppers*, *Velma*, *top quality*; book 8 has *Oh boy*, sentence-final *I fear*, *sticky feets*, *inverted conundrum*, *melty inconvenience*. → Mostly thin-extract noise; most book-7-only markers are 1–2 hits inside a ~620-line window. **Verdict: treat the union as his lexicon, flag the single-hit items as single-hit and note that only *quite*, *goodness*, *toasty*, the honorifics and third-person self-reference are frequent in BOTH books.** Those five are what a generator should lean on; everything else is seasoning, and *he stopped saying mayhaps* is not a claim two extracts can support.

**11. The identity question.** Book 7 does not develop it and its agent correctly refused to invent a line. Book 8 delivers it in one sentence. → Gap filled, no conflict and the right outcome of the merge: the real line instead of a fabricated one.

**Still unresolved and immaterial:** the fourth set of legs. Jamal's own count says five sets by the EMP, the book 7 extract shows three Carl sets breaking plus the EMP, and set 4's end is off-window. Book 8 counts nothing. So *three broke from hopping* is a floor, not a total, and nothing downstream cares.

## Mod implications

Observations, not design decisions — the decisions are chotchki's at B.4.

- **Entity.** Eight double-jointed legs on a body harness is EXACTLY the stock spidertron leg count and topology, so D.1 needs no prototype gymnastics. He is vehicle-scale (17 feet, overweight, does not fit in a truck cab) and rides HIGH (two small characters sit on his head at once), which matters for the raised-body stream origin in D.7 and for where a speech bubble anchors.
- **Two silhouettes, one entity.** Book 7 Jamal is eight plain legs; book 8 Jamal has hand-like grabbers on the front pair and a hat. A sprite that depicts one is picking an era, and it should probably pick book 7's, since that is the era whose legs break.
- **Flamethrower.** Confirmed independently by both books: underbelly mount, aimable nozzle, his primary ranged weapon. Two free gifts: the nozzle direction is a PLOT POINT in book 7 (deliberately pointed backward by order), which is canon cover for per-direction `gun_center_shift`; and it LEAKS tendrils of flame when he is excited, a canonical persistent idle effect rather than an invented one.
- **Animations.** Hop-in-place and front-leg-waving are the canonical excitement idles in both books. The midair flip with a deft landing is in both, so the E.1 airborne pose should read deliberate — he is good at this. The hammerhead shape is the expressive organ: he waves his whole head back and forth to express misery, the beached-loop animation beat. Best rest pose: settling into a circle on the floor with a pet curled against him.
- **Sound.** All canonical and cheap: a whir for the legs running, a click-clack or clank for footfalls on hard ground, and for the break a thrash, a POP, then a mechanical whir plus the smell of burning machinery (not reproducible, but it argues for smoke on the break FX). A yelp or small wordless shout for damage. A whoop and a barrel roll on a kill. Neither narration gives him a shark sound — he TALKS instead, so the speech system IS the sound design.
- **Speech system.** He already writes his own one-liners: 125 verified lines from a windowed extract of two books, median 5–9 words, nearly all surviving truncation to a bubble. The `Jamal is <state>` template generates new faithful lines indefinitely, and mixing grammatical person inside a sentence is more faithful than picking one.
- **Verbosity tiers.** Both books support keying tiers off REGISTER rather than volume alone, and inverted escalation is the finding most likely to get flattened: baroque politeness on the ROUTINE events, plain short words on the rare serious one. A tier system that only prunes frequency loses the joke.
- **Anti-repeat has one canonical exception.** He self-quotes — a line, then the same line later with *indeed* bolted on. An anti-repeat system explicitly ALLOWED to repeat exactly that one reproduces a gag he performs on himself.
- **Break count is the cheapest high-value feature in the character.** Both books count hardware and book 7 counts it OUT LOUD in protest. If the mod tracks break count per entity, the pool can quote the number and the gag improves every time it fires.
- **Two break pools, keyed on fault.** Sheepish when it is his (apologize, defend the hardware, blame himself), outraged when it is not (protest, count, top quality and at the floor the one swear). Both books support the fork, and the mod already knows which case it is: a player-initiated jump is his fault, a biter or a train is not.
- **Damage should mostly be a NOISE.** Both books make the reaction to being hit a yelp, not a sentence. The verbal `damaged` slot is better spent on leg-quality griping than on pain, which he never expresses about his body in either book.
- **Silence is the one thing a speech mod is structurally bad at, and both books need it.** A critically-damaged jamaltron that stops talking entirely is more faithful than any line, because in both books his going quiet is how the text says something is badly wrong.
- **Tone guardrail.** Nobody in two books is ever cruel to him. The player should be annoyed, never encouraged to resent him — which argues for framing the verbosity setting (map-wide since D.3: the bubble is the same for everyone) as mercy for the other players rather than a way to shut him up.
- **What he calls the player is still the biggest open string question**, and it touches every line in the catalog. The FORM is the constant across both books, not the name: *Mister*/*Mr.*/*Miss* + given name, never dropped, applied to literally everyone including animals, with *Gentlemen* for groups.

## Open questions for B.4 (merged and deduped)

Items the merge answered are in the conflicts section above and are not re-asked here.

1. **What he calls the player.** Both books give the same evidence and neither can decide it: always *Mister Carl* (funny, wrong), the player's own name with *Mister*/*Miss* prefixed (faithful to the FORM, the actual constant) or *Gentlemen* for everyone (canonical, and already his group address). This affects every string B.5 generates, so decide it before B.5 runs.
2. **Is the swear shippable, and at what tier?** One line, the funniest thing he says, canonically reserved for maximum misery through no fault of his own. Recommendation: gate it behind the not-his-fault `legs break` at the highest verbosity tier only, never in a common pool. Needs an explicit yes.
3. **`died` has zero anchor in either book.** Invent from his own cheerful *smushed* expectation and book 8's *everything will just stop*, or ship silence. Faithful in spirit and fabricated in letter either way.
4. **Do the quote banks ship as one file or stay split?** Neither: the merged 125 stay LOCAL in `profiles/jamal_quote_bank.md`, alongside `book7_quote_bank.md` and `book8_lines.md` (all gitignored, per SPEC). What reaches the repo is this profile and the tagged catalog in [`lines.md`](lines.md), analysis quotes included.
5. **Verbosity tiers: prune by register or by frequency?** The books give register, not volume. See the inverted-escalation note.
6. **Beached Jamal keeps the weapon or loses it?** Flop speed is closed (slow when wrecked); the weapon is not. Book 8 shows him legless and NOT fighting, which argues for losing it.
7. **Which era does the sprite depict?** Book 7's plain eight legs or book 8's grabber hands and hat.
8. **Does the mod state its era anywhere player-visible?** Framing him as the book-7 prototype rig in the factoriopedia entry or item description is the cheapest way to make the break mechanic read as canon rather than a liberty.
9. **The third-person slip in a one-line bubble.** Slip-plus-correction is the highest-fidelity thing the mod could ship and the only tic that does not survive truncation. Two-line bubbles for that one shape, or drop it?
